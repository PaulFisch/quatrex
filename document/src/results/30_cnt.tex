\section{Carbon nanotubes}
\label{sec:res_cnt}
% ======================================================================

An armchair $(3,3)$ carbon nanotube is tested: a stiff, defect-free
$sp^2$ tube ($36$ DOF per cell, $\sim4$~\AA\ diameter). Its force
constants fit with a larger absolute residual than the silicon wires
(train/test RMSE $0.245$/$0.282$~eV\,\AA$^{-1}$, reflecting the much stiffer
$sp^2$ bonds and hence larger forces) but a clean harmonic spectrum --- no
imaginary modes, and the periodic acoustic sum rule exact (FC3 leg residual
$\sim0$, versus $0.8$ for the open wire). It carries $\sim11$ ballistic channels at
low frequency (versus $\sim4.5$ for \texttt{d11a}), and the SCBA converges to a tight
fixed point (heat-flow conservation $10^{-5}$--$10^{-4}$); driven to a tight
tolerance the one-cell residual reaches $9.6\times10^{-10}$ in $188$
safeguarded-Anderson iterations, giving the same conductance as the loose
($10^{-3}$) stop --- the tube has a single, well-conditioned fixed point.

\paragraph{Temperature.} \Cref{fig:res_cnt_T} shows the converged
conductance from $30$ to $300$~K at a single transport cell. Both the
ballistic and the anharmonic conductance rise with temperature as
channels populate; the anharmonic ratio $G_\mathrm{anh}/G_\mathrm{ball}$
falls monotonically from $0.92$ at $30$~K to $0.71$ at $300$~K, the
signature of three-phonon scattering switching on with the Bose-weighted
phase space and freezing out as $T\to0$.

\paragraph{Length.} At fixed $300$~K the anharmonic ratio decreases with
device length: the converged $G_\mathrm{anh}/G_\mathrm{ball}=0.810,0.702,0.694$ at
one, two and three cells (each to $\le10^{-5}$ with $\lesssim3\times10^{-4}$
heat-flow non-conservation), a clean ballistic-to-diffusive reduction that
accumulates from one to two cells and then nearly saturates. Beyond three cells the
dense reference is too expensive --- the three-cell point already costs $6.8$~h
(\cref{sec:res_scaling}).

\paragraph{Cutoff robustness.}  Across all eight corners of the
(FC3-magnitude, vertex-range, Green's-function-range) cutoff cube,
the converged $G_\mathrm{anh}$ varies by $\pm10\%$ about
the full-coupling value (\cref{fig:res_cnt_cutoff}). This is in contrast to the open
nanowires, where a diagonal-$G$ approximation is
catastrophic (\cref{sec:res_nw}).

\paragraph{The $(8,0)$ zigzag.} As a second, larger tube, we tested
the semiconducting $(8,0)$ zigzag nanotube ($32$ atoms per cell,
the geometry of the electron-transport quatrex example). The fit is clean (test
RMSE $0.151$~eV\,\AA$^{-1}$, about half the $(3,3)$ value thanks to the larger
cell; a soft $0.0014$~THz twist, as befits the wider tube), the ballistic
conductance behaves as expected --- it decreases with length through finite-$\eta$
coherence attenuation, and the larger tube carries more channels at lower per-area
conductance --- and the anharmonic SCBA converges at one cell to
$G_\mathrm{anh}/G_\mathrm{ball}=0.775$, slightly below the $(3,3)$'s $0.810$ (more
channels and more three-phonon phase space). The multi-cell ladder ($192$ DOF at two
cells) exceeds the dense solver's reach and is left to the production path.
