\section{Silicon thin film}
\label{sec:res_qfilm}
% ======================================================================

For a transversely-periodic system, the three-phonon self-energy couples
the transverse momenta by crystal-momentum conservation
(\cref{eq:sigma_phph}). As a quantitative benchmark, we model
cross-plane heat transport through a silicon thin film following~\citet{guo_quantum_2020}:
the film is bulk silicon truncated to $N$ layers along the transport direction with
in-plane two-dimensional periodicity. Conductance
is reported per unit area, $G=J/(A_\perp\Delta T)$, and the cross-plane
conductivity of a film of thickness $L$ is $\kappa(L)=G\,L$.

% REVIEW(verified): the old text attributed eta=0.4 THz to these conductances; that is the PRODUCTION solver's stability floor (sec:prod_open). The numbers of this section are dense-solver runs at small broadening (archived F23/F29: eta_factor 0.1 on the 121-point grid), as recorded in si_film_vs_guo.py. Reworded accordingly.
Unlike the $\Gamma$-only conductors above, the film conductances in this section are
finite-$\eta$: they are dense-solver results at a small but finite broadening
($\eta$-factor $0.1$ on the $121$-point grid), and the $\eta\to0$ fixed-point
machinery of \cref{sec:res_eta0} has not been run for the coupled-$q$ film. The
production coupled-$q$ solver cannot close this gap either --- it is numerically
stable only for $\eta\gtrsim0.4$~THz (\cref{sec:prod_open}). The numbers therefore
carry a residual finite-broadening systematic; since finite $\eta$ weakens the
apparent scattering (\cref{sec:res_approx}), the absolute reductions are likely
under-estimates of the conserving $\eta\to0$ value. They are reported as a
literature benchmark of the coupled-$q$ machinery, not as converged $\eta=0$
transport; reaching $\eta\to0$ in production requires the block-recursion
regularisation noted in \cref{sec:prod_open}.

\paragraph{Ballistic baseline.} The transverse mesh and broadening must
both be converged: at a fixed eight-layer thickness the ballistic
conductance converges for $8\times8$ or finer
($G_\mathrm{ball}=970/928/921/920$~MW\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$ at
$4\times4/8\times8/12\times12/16\times16$), and a small $\eta$ is needed to
avoid the coherence attenuation of \cref{sec:res_nw} that otherwise
crushes the conductance. With these settings the ballistic conductance is
$\sim10^{3}$~MW\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$, declining slowly with thickness
(\cref{fig:res_sifilm_consol}) -- consistent with the scale of
\citet{guo_quantum_2020} for the $2\times2\times2$ force constant.

compares our cross-plane conductance to the anharmonic-NEGF values of

The origin of the over-reduction is not fully resolved. It is, however,
unlikely to lie in the reference's additional numerical approximations: a
first-Born ranking on a three-slab film (coarse $4\times4$ mesh; the relative
ranking, not the absolute value, is the point) finds its diagonal-block
self-energy costs only
$-1.8\%$ and its nearest-neighbour FC3 truncation $+0.0\%$ of the conductance
--- both nearly transport-neutral (a Green's-function-range truncation, by
contrast, is outright unphysical here, cf.\ \cref{sec:res_approx}, and so cannot
account for a mild $16\%$ reduction either). The remaining candidates are the broadening and thin-device
transport setup and the exchange--correlation functional of the underlying
force constants (PBE here vs.\ LDA in the reference). The ballistic conductance is, by contrast,
% REVIEW(verified): at the one matched thickness (3.09 nm) the ballistic gap is 775 vs 928 = 16.5%; "~15%" tightened to ~16%.
force-constant sensitive at the $\sim16\%$ level (\cref{fig:res_sifilm_consol}):
the $2\times2\times2$ QE--FD constant -- comparable to the reference's own
QE harmonic input -- matches their scale, but the higher-quality
$5\times5\times5$ VASP constant gives a $\sim16\%$ lower ballistic ($775$ vs
$928$ at $3.09$~nm; purely
a harmonic difference, the slightly softer dispersion). So the agreement
with the reference's ballistic value holds only for the $2\times2\times2$
constant, not force-constant-independently.

\paragraph{Long-range force constant.} The thin-film
results above use the bulk force constants from a small
$2\times2\times2$ supercell with FD. To test convergence, we generated a substantially
higher-quality force constant using hiphive on a $5\times5\times5$
primitive supercell ($250$ atoms) from $24$ rattled
VASP-PBE configurations, with a third-order cutoff extended to
$\sim$fourth-neighbour ($5.0$~\AA). The fit is good (automatic
relevance determination, $103$ parameters, train/test RMSE both
$0.020$~eV\,\AA$^{-1}$), the rotational and acoustic sum rules are
satisfied, and the dispersion looks correct. On this force
constant, the cross-plane scaling (\cref{fig:res_sifilm_consol}) gives
$G_\mathrm{anh}/G_\mathrm{ball}=0.52,0.45,0.38$ at $1.16,1.93,3.09$~nm:
the anharmonic reduction grows with thickness,
and is larger than with the short-cutoff force constant because
the longer-range third-order terms add inter-slab scattering. The
over-reduction relative to \citet{guo_quantum_2020} therefore persists with the
better-converged force constant. The long-range constant is also the more
\emph{numerically} robust one: its anharmonic SCBA converges cleanly at
every thickness, whereas the short-cutoff $2\times2\times2$ constant
destabilises the small-broadening SCBA away from a single thickness
(hence its anharmonic value is shown at one point only in
\cref{fig:res_sifilm_consol}).

\section{Silicon/germanium heterostructure}
\label{sec:res_hetero}
% ======================================================================

A Ge-layer barrier embedded in a silicon film (Si force constants,
Ge mass; Si contacts) is built with a per-slab mass profile threaded
through the $q_\perp$-resolved driver (an all-Si profile reproduces the
homogeneous driver to machine precision). The mass-mismatch barrier
raises the ballistic thermal resistance by $+344$--$369\%$.
The phonon-phonon enhancement of the resistance is present in the pure
silicon film -- $+32\%$ at $2.3$~nm growing to $+51\%$ at $5.4$~nm -- but is
nearly neutral ($\pm1\%$) in the barrier-dominated heterostructure. A
plausible interpretation, not separately verified, is that the strong
barrier filters the transmission to low-frequency acoustic phonons that are
anharmonically long-lived, so three-phonon scattering adds little resistance
through the barrier.
