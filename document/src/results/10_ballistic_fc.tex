\section{Ballistic Phonon NEGF}
\label{sec:res_ballistic}
% ======================================================================

As a heterogeneous test, we model a ballistic Si/Ge interface in the
mass-mismatch approximation, as done in \cite{latour_ab_2017, tian_enhancing_2012, guo_quantum_2020}: bulk-Si force constants in the whole device, with
the Si/Ge distinction entering only through the atomic masses
($m_\mathrm{Si}=28.09$, $m_\mathrm{Ge}=72.64$~amu). At $\Gamma$, the Si
optical maximum is $15.55$~THz and the Ge mass-clone gives $9.67$~THz,
consistent with $f_\mathrm{Ge}=f_\mathrm{Si}\sqrt{m_\mathrm{Si}/m_\mathrm{Ge}}$.
On a $20\times20$ transverse mesh, the interfacial conductance at
$300$~K is $G=196$~MW\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$, within $\sim7\%$ of the
literature values ($210$, Latour 2017; $209$, Tian 2012). the
remaining gap is consistent with the coarser transverse mesh and DFT calculations.

\section{Force Constants}

\paragraph{Method cross-check.} Bulk Silicon harmonic force constants
computed by density-functional perturbation theory (QE \texttt{ph.x}
$\to$ \texttt{q2r.x}) and by finite displacement (phono3py + symfc) on
the same primitive cell agree to $1.6\%$ on the zone-centre optical
frequency ($15.12$ vs $15.37$~THz; \cref{fig:res_fcmethod}). DFPT third-order
constants via \texttt{d3q} require a norm-conserving pseudopotential, and can hence
not be calculated to the same accuracy as with finite-displacement methods.

\paragraph{Bulk Si $\kappa$.} As an independent check of the force-constant
pipeline and the physics, separate from the NEGF solver, the finite-displacement
FC3 is fed to a phono3py relaxation-time-approximation Boltzmann calculation. The
result is essentially mesh-converged, bulk-silicon
$\kappa(300\,\mathrm{K})=110.7$~W\,m$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$ on an $11^3$ $q$-mesh and
$108.7$ on $19^3$, $\sim25\%$ below the experimental $\sim150$, consistent with the
coarse $2\times2\times2$ FC3 supercell (cutoff $5.5$~\AA). Sweeping temperature with
natural-isotope scattering gives a direct comparison to the classic
Glassbrenner--Slack single-crystal data (\cref{tab:res_kappaT}): the calculation
reproduces the experimental temperature dependence -- the high-temperature
$\kappa\propto1/T$ fall-off is captured (the conductivity halves between $300$ and
$600$~K) -- and lies $\sim20$--$30\%$ low in magnitude, the deficit growing toward
low temperature where long-range cubic and isotope terms matter most. Both effects
point to the under-converged $2\times2\times2$ FC3 supercell.

\paragraph{FC3 compression.} The tensor-decomposition methods of
\cref{sub:fc3_compression} are applied to the device FC3 of the
\texttt{d11a} nanowire ($39.4\times10^6$ dense parameters) and the
compressed tensors fed back through the full anharmonic transport solve
(\cref{fig:res_decomp}). We observe that, first, transport
observable converges faster than the Frobenius residual the fitters
minimise: the symmetric ans\"atze (INDSCAL, Waring) reach a $\sim15\%$
conductance error at rank $16$ ($\sim3600$--$4500\times$ fewer
parameters) while their Frobenius residual is still $52$--$67\%$. The
relevant fitting target for phonon NEGF is therefore a transport
residual, not the tensor norm. Second, the current compression approach
has distinct failure modes: the
matricised SVD is both far denser (its factor count is $10$--$100\times$
the symmetric methods') and unphysical at higher rank, with its
rank-8 fit violating heat-flow conservation and its rank-16 fit giving a
negative $G_\mathrm{anh}$. Conversely, the Tucker (HOSVD) and INDSCAL fits at
the lowest ranks collapse to the bare ballistic value. Only the symmetric INDSCAL/Waring families degrade
