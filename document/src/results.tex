\section{Ballistic Phonon NEGF}
\label{sec:res_ballistic}
% ======================================================================

As a heterogeneous test, we model a ballistic Si/Ge interface in the
mass-mismatch approximation, as done in \cite{latour_ab_2017, tian_enhancing_2012, guo_quantum_2020}: bulk-Si force constants in the whole device, with
the Si/Ge distinction entering only through the atomic masses
($m_\mathrm{Si}=28.09$, $m_\mathrm{Ge}=72.64$~amu). At $\Gamma$, the Si
optical maximum is $15.55$~THz and the Ge mass-clone gives $9.67$~THz,
consistent with $f_\mathrm{Ge}=f_\mathrm{Si}\sqrt{m_\mathrm{Si}/m_\mathrm{Ge}}$.
On a $20\times20$ transverse mesh, the interfacial conductance at
$300$~K is $G=196$~MW\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$, within $\sim7\%$ of the
literature values ($210$, Latour 2017; $209$, Tian 2012). the
remaining gap is consistent with the coarser transverse mesh and DFT calculations.

\section{Force Constants}
\label{sec:res_fc}
% ======================================================================

\paragraph{Method cross-check.} Bulk Silicon harmonic force constants
computed by density-functional perturbation theory (QE \texttt{ph.x}
$\to$ \texttt{q2r.x}) and by finite displacement (phono3py + symfc) on
the same primitive cell agree to $1.6\%$ on the zone-centre optical
frequency ($15.12$ vs $15.37$~THz; \cref{fig:res_fcmethod}). DFPT third-order
constants via \texttt{d3q} require a norm-conserving pseudopotential, and can hence
not be calculated to the same accuracy as with finite-displacement methods.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.6\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/fc_method_dispersion_si.pdf}
  \caption{Bulk-silicon phonon dispersion from DFPT versus
  finite-displacement force constants.}
  \label{fig:res_fcmethod}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Bulk Si $\kappa$.} The finite-displacement FC3 as input to a
phono3py RTA-BTE calculation gives bulk-silicon
$\kappa(300\,\mathrm{K})=110.7$~W\,m$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$ on an $11^3$ mesh and
$108.7$ on $19^3$. This is
$\sim25\%$ below the experimental $\sim150$, likely due to the coarse $2\times2\times2$
FC3 supercell (cutoff $5.5$~\AA). \todo[inline]{Rerun this with the better Si FC3}

\paragraph{FC3 compression.} The tensor-decomposition methods of
\cref{sec:fc3_compression} are applied to the device FC3 of the
\texttt{d11a} nanowire ($39.4\times10^6$ dense parameters) and the
compressed tensors fed back through the full anharmonic transport solve
(\cref{fig:res_decomp}). We observe that, first, transport
observable converges faster than the Frobenius residual the fitters
minimise: the symmetric ans\"atze (INDSCAL, Waring) reach a $\sim15\%$
conductance error at rank $16$ ($\sim3600$--$4500\times$ fewer
parameters) while their Frobenius residual is still $52$--$67\%$. The
relevant fitting target for phonon NEGF is therefore a transport
residual, not the tensor norm. Second, the current compression approach
has distinct failure modes: the
matricised SVD is both far denser (its factor count is $10$--$100\times$
the symmetric methods') and unphysical at higher rank, with its
rank-8 fit violating heat-flow conservation and its rank-16 fit giving a
negative $G_\mathrm{anh}$. Conversely, the Tucker (HOSVD) and INDSCAL fits at
the lowest ranks collapse to the bare ballistic value. Only the symmetric INDSCAL/Waring families degrade
gracefully

\section{Anharmonic transport in silicon nanowires}
\label{sec:res_nw}
% ======================================================================

We study two hydrogen-passivated [100] silicon nanowires: \texttt{d5a}
($\mathrm{Si}_9\mathrm{H}_{12}$ per cell, $63$ DOF) and the wider
\texttt{d11a} ($135$ DOF). Their harmonic transport is physical
(\cref{fig:res_ballistic_curves})\todo[inline]{Remove from the plot the cnt or fold the cnt}: the ballistic conductance rises with
temperature, and \texttt{d11a} carries
$\sim1.5\times$ the conductance of \texttt{d5a}.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/ballistic_vs_T_d5_d11.pdf}\hfill
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/ballistic_vs_length_d5_d11.pdf}
  \caption{Ballistic conductance of the \texttt{d5a} and \texttt{d11a}
  silicon nanowires. Left: $G(T)$; right: $G$ versus device length.}
  \label{fig:res_ballistic_curves}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Mode-resolved anharmonic physics.} A phono3py analysis of the
bulk-silicon FC3 (\cref{fig:res_modephysics}) is performed: phonon lifetimes
have a median of $4.2$~ps with a $\tau\!\propto\!\omega^{-2}$ envelope.
$50\%$ of $\kappa$ is carried by phonons with mean free path $>134$~nm
and $93\%$ by acoustic modes. Umklapp accounts for $0.44$ of the scattering at $300$~K,
and the mode Gr\"uneisen parameters have mean $|\gamma|=1.04$, matching the
experimental Si value and validating the FC3 anharmonicity strength.
Only $1.3\%$ of bulk $\kappa$ comes from phonons with mean free path below $10$~nm. The
$\sim1$~nm nanowire cores therefore sit far below the bulk
phonon-MFP spectrum: they are mostly performing boundary scattering and
retain almost none of the bulk anharmonic-limited $\kappa$.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/phph_physics_si.pdf}\hfill
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/phph_NU_gruneisen_si.pdf}
  \caption{Bulk-silicon mode-resolved anharmonic physics (phono3py,
  FD FC3). Left: lifetimes, $\kappa$-accumulation versus mean free path,
  and the acoustic-mode $\kappa$ fraction. Right: Normal/Umklapp split
  and the mode Gr\"uneisen distribution (mean $|\gamma|=1.04$).}
  \label{fig:res_modephysics}
\end{figure}

At physical broadening and moderate vertex strength the SCBA converges
cleanly and gives the physical $G_\mathrm{anh}<G_\mathrm{ball}$.

A self-energy-level cutoff sweep on the \texttt{d5a} and \texttt{d11a}
nanowires (\cref{fig:res_cutoffs}) quantifies the truncations: the FC3
magnitude threshold converges quickly for \texttt{d5a}
($10^{-3}\to0.7\%$ relative change in $\Sigma^<$) but the wider
\texttt{d11a} needs a tighter threshold. A diagonal-$G$ approximation inside the
bubble is catastrophic ($5\times$ for \texttt{d5a}, $30\times$ for
\texttt{d11a}). The dominant cutoff is the Green's-function range, not
the vertex magnitude.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.6\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/cutoff_sse_d5_d11.pdf}
  \caption{Self-energy sensitivity to the FC3 magnitude cutoff and to a
  diagonal-$G$ approximation for the \texttt{d5a} and \texttt{d11a}
  nanowires. The off-diagonal Green's-function content dominates.}
  \label{fig:res_cutoffs}
\end{figure}

Perofrming a rattle-amplitude sweep for the
\texttt{d5a} nanowire, the twist frequency stays real and stable
($0.5$--$1.0$~THz at $q_z=0.02$) at every amplitude ($0.015$--$0.08$~\AA),
with only fit-noise scatter --- never imaginary. The \texttt{d5a} twist is thus
robust.

\section{Carbon nanotubes}
\label{sec:res_cnt}
% ======================================================================

An armchair $(3,3)$ carbon nanotube is tested: a stiff, defect-free
$sp^2$ tube ($36$ DOF per cell, $\sim4$~\AA\ diameter) whose force
constants fit cleanly (train/test RMSE $0.020$~eV\,\AA$^{-1}$, no
imaginary modes) and whose periodic acoustic sum rule is exact. It
carries $\sim11$ ballistic channels at low frequency (versus $\sim4.5$
for \texttt{d11a}), and the SCBA converges to a tight fixed point
(residual $<10^{-5}$, heat-flow conservation $10^{-5}$--$10^{-4}$).

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/cnt33_temperature_g.pdf}\hfill
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/cnt33_temperature_ratio.pdf}
  \caption{Anharmonic transport of the $(3,3)$ carbon nanotube
  versus temperature (single transport cell). \emph{Left}: ballistic and
  anharmonic per-area conductance. \emph{Right}: the ratio
  $G_\mathrm{anh}/G_\mathrm{ball}$ rises toward unity as $T\to0$.}
  \label{fig:res_cnt_T}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/cnt33_cutoff.pdf}
  \caption{$(3,3)$ carbon nanotube with two transport cells: $G_\mathrm{anh}$ across the
   self-energy combinations (FC3 magnitude $\sigma$,
   vertex range $v$, Green's-function range $g$).
  All lie within $\pm10\%$ of the full-coupling value (dashed).}
  \label{fig:res_cnt_cutoff}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Temperature.} \Cref{fig:res_cnt_T} shows the converged
conductance from $30$ to $300$~K at a single transport cell. Both the
ballistic and the anharmonic conductance rise with temperature as
channels populate; the anharmonic ratio $G_\mathrm{anh}/G_\mathrm{ball}$
falls monotonically from $0.92$ at $30$~K to $0.71$ at $300$~K, the
signature of three-phonon scattering switching on with the Bose-weighted
phase space and freezing out as $T\to0$.

\paragraph{Length.} At fixed $300$~K the anharmonic ratio decreases with
device length: $G_\mathrm{anh}/G_\mathrm{ball}=0.81,0.70,0.69$ at one,
two and three cells. Beyond three cells, the
dense reference solver is too expensive.

\paragraph{Cutoff robustness.}  Across all eight corners of the
(FC3-magnitude, vertex-range, Green's-function-range) cutoff cube,
the converged $G_\mathrm{anh}$ varies by $\pm10\%$ about
the full-coupling value (\cref{fig:res_cnt_cutoff}). This is in contrast to the open
nanowires, where a diagonal-$G$ approximation is
catastrophic (\cref{sec:res_cutoffs}).

\paragraph{The $(8,0)$ zigzag.} As a second, larger tube, we tested
the semiconducting $(8,0)$ zigzag nanotube ($32$ atoms per cell,
the geometry of the electron-transport quatrex example). The fit is clean and
the ballistic conductance behaves
as expected --- it decreases with length through finite-$\eta$ coherence
attenuation, and the larger tube carries more channels at lower per-area
conductance. \todo[inline]{Add results with quatrex}

\section{Silicon thin film}
\label{sec:res_qfilm}
% ======================================================================

For a transversely-periodic system, the three-phonon self-energy couples
the transverse momenta by crystal-momentum conservation
(\cref{eq:sigma_guo}). As a quantitative benchmark, we model
cross-plane heat transport through a silicon thin film following~\citet{guo_quantum_2020}:
the film is bulk silicon truncated to $N$ layers along the transport direction with
in-plane two-dimensional periodicity. Conductance
is reported per unit area, $G=J/(A_\perp\Delta T)$, and the cross-plane
conductivity of a film of thickness $L$ is $\kappa(L)=G\,L$.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/si_film_conductance_a.pdf}\hfill
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/si_film_conductance_b.pdf}
  \caption{Cross-plane heat transport through a silicon thin film at
  $300$~K with bulk-silicon force constants (transverse mesh $8\times8$,
  $121$ frequencies). \emph{Left}: per-area conductance --
  our ballistic and anharmonic NEGF against \citet{guo_quantum_2020}.
  \emph{Right}: the ballistic $\kappa=G_\mathrm{ball}L$ rises linearly
  with thickness.}
  \label{fig:res_sifilm}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Ballistic baseline.} The transverse mesh and broadening must
both be converged: at a fixed eight-layer thickness the ballistic
conductance converges for $8\times8$ or finer
($G_\mathrm{ball}=970/928/921/920$~MW\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$ at
$4\times4/8\times8/12\times12/16\times16$), and a small $\eta$ is needed to
avoid the coherence attenuation of \cref{sec:res_nw} that otherwise
crushes the conductance. With these settings the ballistic conductance is
$\sim10^{3}$~MW\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$, declining slowly with thickness
(\cref{fig:res_sifilm_consol}) -- consistent with the scale of
\citet{guo_quantum_2020} for the $2\times2\times2$ force constant.

\paragraph{Anharmonic conductance.} \Cref{fig:res_sifilm} (left)
compares our cross-plane conductance to the anharmonic-NEGF values of
Guo~\emph{et al.} at $3$ and $5$ unit cells. The three-phonon scattering
reduces the $3$-unit-cell conductance by $\sim45\%$
($G_\mathrm{anh}\approx570$~MW\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$), significantly more than the
reference's $\sim10\%$ ($G_\mathrm{anh}=939.7$). This might be due to the reference's
additional self-energy approximations (the diagonal-block self-energy
and the nearest-neighbour FC3 truncation quantified in
\cref{sec:res_approx}), with the broadening and thin-device transport
setup, and with the exchange--correlation functional of the underlying
force constants (PBE here). The ballistic conductance is, by contrast,
force-constant sensitive at the $\sim15\%$ level (\cref{fig:res_sifilm_consol}):
the $2\times2\times2$ QE--FD constant -- comparable to the reference's own
QE harmonic input -- matches their scale, but the higher-quality
$5\times5\times5$ VASP constant gives a $\sim15\%$ lower ballistic (purely
a harmonic difference, the slightly softer dispersion). So the agreement
with the reference's ballistic value holds only for the $2\times2\times2$
constant, not force-constant-independently.

\paragraph{long-range force constant.} The thin-film
results above use the bulk force constants from a small
$2\times2\times2$ supercell with FD. To test convergence, we generated a substantially
higher-quality force constant using hiphive on a $5\times5\times5$
primitive supercell ($250$ atoms) from $24$ rattled
VASP-PBE configurations, with a third-order cutoff extended to
$\sim$fourth-neighbour ($5.0$~\AA). The fit is good (automatic
relevance determination, $103$ parameters, train/test RMSE both
$0.020$~eV\,\AA$^{-1}$), the rotational and acoustic sum rules are
satisfied, and the dispersion looks correct. On this force
constant, the cross-plane scaling (\cref{fig:res_sifilm_consol}) gives
$G_\mathrm{anh}/G_\mathrm{ball}=0.52,0.45,0.38$ at $1.16,1.93,3.09$~nm:
the anharmonic reduction grows with thickness,
and is larger than with the short-cutoff force constant because
the longer-range third-order terms add inter-slab scattering. The
over-reduction relative to \citet{guo_quantum_2020} therefore persists with the
better-converged force constant. The long-range constant is also the more
\emph{numerically} robust one: its anharmonic SCBA converges cleanly at
every thickness, whereas the short-cutoff $2\times2\times2$ constant
destabilises the small-broadening SCBA away from a single thickness
(hence its anharmonic value is shown at one point only in
\cref{fig:res_sifilm_consol}).

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/si_film_vs_guo.pdf}
  \caption{Consolidated cross-plane silicon thin-film conductance vs.\
  thickness at $300$~K ($8\times8$ mesh, $121$ frequencies): our
  ballistic and anharmonic NEGF with the $2\times2\times2$ (QE--FD) and
  the high-quality $5\times5\times5$ ($\sim$fourth-neighbour, VASP)
  force constants, against the anharmonic values of
  \citet{guo_quantum_2020} ($939.7$ at $3$~uc, $890.97$ at $5$~uc; their

  \label{fig:res_sifilm_consol}
\end{figure}

\section{Silicon/germanium heterostructure}
\label{sec:res_hetero}
% ======================================================================

A Ge-layer barrier embedded in a silicon film (Si force constants,
Ge mass; Si contacts) is built with a per-slab mass profile threaded
through the $q_\perp$-resolved driver (an all-Si profile reproduces the
homogeneous driver to machine precision). The mass-mismatch barrier
raises the ballistic thermal resistance by $+340$--$370\%$.
The phonon-phonon enhancement of the resistance is present in the pure
silicon film -- $+32\%$ at $2.3$~nm growing to $+51\%$ at $5.4$~nm -- but is suppressed
($\lesssim1\%$) in the heterostructure.

\section{Loop and Tadpole}
\label{sec:res_scp}
% ======================================================================

Beyond the dynamic three-phonon bubble $\Sigma_B(\omega)$ (the only term
with an imaginary part, hence the linewidth), the lowest-order diagrammatic expansion
adds two static, real, Hermitian self-energies: the quartic loop
$\Sigma_L=\tfrac12\,\Phi_4\!:\!\langle uu\rangle$ (needs FC4) and the cubic tadpole
$\Sigma_T=\Phi_3\!:\!\langle u\rangle$ with
$\langle u\rangle=-\Phi_\mathrm{eff}^{-1}\,\tfrac12\Phi_3\!:\!\langle
uu\rangle$ (internal-coordinate relaxation, needs FC3). We computed all
three contributions across temperature for the soft-mode
\texttt{d5a} nanowire and the stiff (3,3) carbon nanotube
(\cref{fig:res_scp_mag}). The CNT FC4 was obtained from the existing
rattled set with a short ($2.0$~\AA) quartic cutoff,
the \texttt{d5a} FC4 from the \texttt{rfe} refit of \cref{sec:res_fc}.

For the CNT, the dynamic bubble dominates,
$\max|\mathrm{Re}\,\Sigma_B|:\|\Sigma_L\|:\|\Sigma_T\|\approx 8:1:0.5$
at $300$~K, all growing with $T$ as $\langle uu\rangle$ grows. For the
\texttt{d5a} wire, the ordering inverts: the bubble is weak
($\max|\mathrm{Re}\,\Sigma_B|\!\approx\!670$~THz$^2$ at $300$~K), while the
loop is the largest correction
($\|\Sigma_L\|\!\approx\!1045$~THz$^2$). In both structures, the loop
exceeds the tadpole at every temperature.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/static_se_study.pdf}
  \caption{Static-correction magnitudes (top) and heat-flow conservation
  per correction (bottom) versus temperature, for the CNT (left)
  and the \texttt{d5a} nanowire (right)}
  \label{fig:res_scp_mag}
\end{figure}

For the CNT, the loop is a $\sim10\%$ renormalisation that does not improve the
($10^{-4}$) conservation, so it is unnecessary. For \texttt{d5a}, it is the
dominant static term. It makes the high-temperature SCBA converge, and it improves the
heat-flow conservation (halving it from $2.98\%$ to $1.34\%$ at $30$~K,
and reaching $0.45\%$ at $100$~K with the loop$+$tadpole). The loop is the
physical cure for a soft mode: stiffening it lifts the propagator out of
the near-singular region.

Run in isolation, the tadpole diverges: for \texttt{d5a} the
tadpole-only self-energy reaches $\|\Sigma_T\|=1298$~THz$^2$ at $300$~K and drives the lowest mode imaginary ($\omega^2=-2.10$), with the SCBA failing and the heat-flow
conservation increasing. The optical tadpole
$T_O$ is the gradient that relaxes the internal coordinates and
vanishes at the relaxed structure~\cite{paulatto_anharmonic_2013}.
With coupled self-consistency, the quartic loop
stiffens the modes, which lowers $\langle uu\rangle$, which shrinks
$\langle u\rangle$ and hence $\Sigma_T$. Running loop and tadpole together
(as SSCHA does~\cite{tadano_first-principles_2018}) the same $300$~K point gives
$\|\Sigma_T\|=284$~THz$^2$ ($4.6\times$ smaller) and a stable
lowest mode ($0.765$~THz). On bulk silicon, $\|\Sigma_T\|$ stays at $1$--$7$~THz$^2$
(a few percent of the optical $\omega^2$) across $100$--$600$~K and never
destabilises.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/static_se_tadpole_breakdown.pdf}
  \caption{Tadpole magnitudes versus temperature for the CNT and
  \texttt{d5a}. Circled points mark where the tadpole-only self-energy
  drives an imaginary renormalised mode and the SCBA does not converge.}
  \label{fig:res_scp_tadpole}
\end{figure}

\section{Self-consistent convergence at vanishing broadening}
\label{sec:res_eta0}
% ======================================================================

The numerical broadening $\eta$ in \cref{eq:system} regularises the contact and
device Green's functions but is not physical; a conserving anharmonic
conductance is defined only in the $\eta\to0$ limit (\cref{sub:eta_zero}). We
examined whether that limit is reachable directly, using the
extrapolation/root-finding solvers of \cref{sub:rootfinders} with the smooth
spectral window and infrared taper. The two test structures behave oppositely,
and the boundary between them is set by a single, a~priori computable quantity:
the anharmonic linewidth relative to the frequency grid.

\paragraph{CNT: a genuine $\eta=0$ fixed point.}
For the armchair (3,3) tube the $\eta=0$ SCBA converges to a true fixed point.
With the smooth window, the infrared taper, the causal (Kramers--Kronig)
retarded self-energy and plain linear or reduced-rank-extrapolation mixing at
$\eta=10^{-12}$, the two-cell device reaches a relative $\Sigma^R$ residual of
$3.8\times10^{-11}$, a lead-current imbalance
$|J_L-J_R|/|J|=3.2\times10^{-11}$, and an energy-balance residual
(\cref{eq:bubble_balance}) of $1.8\times10^{-16}$ in $49$ iterations
(\cref{fig:res_eta0_conv}, left) -- a self-consistent, current-conserving
solution at no broadening. The physical conductance is the grid-weighted heat
current $G\cdot\mathrm{d}\omega\approx 17$ (the unweighted lead sum scales with
the number of grid points and is not itself the conductance). It is insensitive
at the few-percent level to the infrared regularisation: across
$\omega_{\rm reg}=1.83$--$3.06$~THz the converged $G\cdot\mathrm{d}\omega$ varies
by $3.7\%$ and extrapolates to $17.4$ as $\omega_{\rm reg}\to0$
(\cref{fig:res_eta0_cutoff}, left), and it changes by $1\%$ between the $181$-
and $361$-point grids (right).

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/eta0_convergence_methods.pdf}
  \caption{$\eta=0$ SCBA convergence. Left: the (3,3) CNT reaches a genuine
  fixed point -- the $\Sigma^R$ residual and lead imbalance fall to
  $\sim 10^{-11}$ while the energy balance stays at $10^{-16}$. Right: for the

\paragraph{Anharmonic transport from the converged solution.} The conserving
$\eta=0$ fixed point yields a genuine inelastic conductance. The ballistic
transmission carries up to $\sim 12$ channels with the expected band structure;
three-phonon scattering suppresses it across the whole band
(\cref{fig:res_eta0_trans}), removing $39\%$ of the band-integrated transmission
at two transport cells and $46\%$ at three -- the resistive onset grows with
length. The ballistic conductance is itself length-independent (the Landauer
baseline, $8.0\times10^{8}$~W\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$ at two, three and four cells), so
the falling anharmonic ratio is genuine scattering, not coherence loss. The
frequency-integrated $G_\mathrm{anh}/G_\mathrm{ball}$ (\cref{fig:res_eta0_transport})
rises modestly from $0.75$ at $30$~K to $\approx 0.81$ near $100$~K and then falls
to $0.57$ at $300$~K as the three-phonon scattering rate grows with the thermal
phonon population, and it deepens with device length, $0.57\to0.51$ from two to
three transport cells at $300$~K (the four-cell anharmonic run did not converge
at $\eta=0$ and is excluded). These production-NEGF values at $\eta=10^{-12}$ are
obtained with a different solver and broadening than the dense-reference numbers
of \cref{sec:res_cnt} (a monotonic $0.92\to0.71$); they show a stronger reduction
but the same qualitative increase of anharmonic resistance with temperature and
length.

\paragraph{d5a: a marginal fixed point at finite grid.}
The hydrogen-passivated \texttt{d5a} wire behaves differently because of its
surface modes. The golden-rule linewidth (\cref{eq:fgr}), evaluated directly from
$\Phi^{(3)}$ with no SCBA, separates cleanly by hydrogen character
(\cref{fig:res_eta0_gamma}): the silicon acoustic and optical modes
($0$--$15$~THz) have $\Gamma_{\rm anh}\approx4$~THz, far above any feasible grid
spacing; the Si--H bending band ($\approx 16$--$21$~THz, $\sim 90\%$ hydrogen character)
has a median $\Gamma_{\rm anh}\approx 0.65$~THz but a tail down to
$\approx 0.13$~THz; and the Si--H stretch ($\approx 63$~THz) has
$\Gamma_{\rm anh}\approx 0.13$~THz but carries no heat -- its occupation is
frozen and it is masked. The sharp, heat-carrying tail of the bending band thus
straddles the grid spacing ($\mathrm{d}\omega=0.37$ and $0.18$~THz at $181$ and
$361$ points), and by \cref{eq:resolvent_gain} it renders the $\eta=0$ fixed
point marginal.

Consistent with this, no iterative solver reaches it (\cref{fig:res_eta0_conv},
right). Plain and reduced-rank mixing limit-cycle; RPM diverges, with a measured
unstable-subspace radius $|\lambda(H)|\approx 28$ ($3$ unstable modes) at $181$
points growing to $\approx 200$ ($5$ modes) at $361$ points -- the instability
strengthens as the grid resolves the sharp modes, confirming it is physical
rather than a discretisation artifact. Jacobian-free Newton--Krylov stays bounded
(its trust region prevents the RPM blow-up) and reduces the residual from unity
to $\approx 0.3$ ($0.285$ at $k{=}25$, $0.522$ at $k{=}50$), but then stalls:
independently of the Krylov dimension the inner GMRES cannot reduce the Newton
residual below $\sim 97\%$ of its value -- the signature of a near-singular,
indefinite Newton Jacobian. We therefore did not obtain a converged, conserving $\eta=0$
conductance for \texttt{d5a}. A fixed broadening makes the self-energy converge
but breaks conservation in proportion to $\eta$ (the lead imbalance is $2.2$ at
$\eta=0.37$~THz and $1.2$ at $0.18$~THz), so the physically meaningful number
would have to come from a regularise-then-extrapolate sequence $\eta\to0$, which
we have not carried out. The boundary is sharp and predictive: the $\eta=0$
anharmonic transport problem is well posed exactly when every heat-carrying mode
is resolved by the frequency grid.

\section{Strong anharmonicity: SrTiO\textsubscript{3}}
\label{sec:res_strong}
% ======================================================================

The static corrections of \cref{sec:res_scp} are a small perturbation for the
weakly anharmonic silicon systems. To test the opposite, strongly anharmonic
regime, where the harmonic approximation fails, we computed
FC2/FC3/FC4 for cubic SrTiO$_3$, the canonical
self-consistent-phonon (SCP) test case from~\cite{tadano_first-principles_2018}.

Cubic SrTiO$_3$ has unstable harmonic phonons: a ferroelectric (FE) mode at $\Gamma$ and an
antiferrodistortive (AFD) octahedral-rotation mode at
$R=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$~\cite{tadano_first-principles_2018}. Fitting FC2 to
small-amplitude ($0.025$~\AA) rattled displacements in the same $2\times2\times2$
($40$-atom) cell used by Tadano \& Tsuneyuki recovers the AFD instability:
$\omega_R = 55i$--$92i$~cm$^{-1}$ (converging upward with the number of training
structures, an instability that strengthens as the fit
converges). Their PBEsol value is $76i$~cm$^{-1}$. The static loop$+$tadpole magnitude is
$\|\Sigma_\mathrm{static}\|\approx39$~THz$^2$ at $300$~K, $\sim6\times$ the bulk-Si
value.

Fitting the same cell to a larger ($0.08$~\AA) rattle amplitude stabilises the AFD mode
($\omega_R = +90$~cm$^{-1}$, \cref{fig:res_srtio3}). This is not a fitting error,
but the central identity of the effective-potential methods: a finite-amplitude fit
returns the effective, renormalised FC2 at the temperature where
$\langle u^2\rangle^{1/2}$ matches the rattle amplitude. Fitting at the thermal
amplitude is exactly what the temperature-dependent effective potential
(TDEP)~\cite{hellman_temperature_2013} and the stochastic self-consistent harmonic
approximation (SSCHA)~\cite{monacelli_stochastic_2021} do. The difference between
the bare and effective FC2 is the static loop self-energy
$\tfrac12\Phi_4\!:\!\langle uu\rangle$.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/srtio3_rattle_renorm.pdf}
  \caption{SrTiO$_3$ phonon dispersion: FC2 fitted to a small ($0.025$~\AA, red)
  versus large ($0.08$~\AA, blue) rattle amplitude.}
  \label{fig:res_srtio3}
\end{figure}

The renormalised phonons can hence be obtained either from the bare FC2 plus an
explicit quartic loop (the deterministic SCP of \cref{sec:res_scp}, requiring
FC4), or directly by fitting the effective FC2 at the thermal amplitude
(TDEP/SSCHA, no FC4 needed)~\cite{hellman_temperature_2013,monacelli_stochastic_2021}.
The explicit loop is needed when an analytic temperature dependence from a single
FC4 is wanted. The effective-fit route is preferred when FC4 is too costly.
For the weakly anharmonic silicon systems neither matters (effective $\approx$ bare FC2).
For SrTiO$_3$ one must use one route or the other.
