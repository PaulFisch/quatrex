\chapter{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This chapter collects the validation and production measurements. The
harmonic backbone is checked first (\cref{sec:res_ballistic}), then the
force-constant inputs (\cref{sec:res_fc}), the anharmonic transport on
finite silicon nanowires (\cref{sec:res_nw}), the transversely-periodic
$q_\perp$-resolved anharmonic problem and the silicon thin-film benchmark
(\cref{sec:res_qfilm}), the verification of the absolute
three-phonon self-energy prefactor (\cref{sec:res_prefactor}), the cost
of the numerical approximations (\cref{sec:res_approx}), a Si/Ge
heterostructure (\cref{sec:res_hetero}), and the distributed-solver and
GPU-memory scaling of the implementation
(\cref{sec:res_scaling}). Implementation mechanisms referenced here are
described in \cref{ssec:phph_distribution,ssec:phph_qdist}; the dense
reference solver used for the standalone studies is documented in
\cref{app:convergence_diagnosis}.

% ======================================================================
\section{Ballistic phonon NEGF validation}
\label{sec:res_ballistic}
% ======================================================================

The harmonic transport backbone is validated against an independent
Sancho--Rubio decimation plus Caroli reference on identical harmonic
inputs, transverse mesh, and frequency grid. For homogeneous bulk
silicon the two transmissions agree, confirming the phonopy block
extraction, the block-tridiagonal assembly, and the open-boundary
treatment.

As a heterogeneous test we model a ballistic Si/Ge interface in the
mass-mismatch approximation: bulk-Si force constants throughout, with
the Si/Ge distinction entering only through the atomic masses
($m_\mathrm{Si}=28.09$, $m_\mathrm{Ge}=72.64$~amu). At $\Gamma$ the Si
optical maximum is $15.55$~THz and the Ge mass-clone gives $9.67$~THz,
consistent with $f_\mathrm{Ge}=f_\mathrm{Si}\sqrt{m_\mathrm{Si}/m_\mathrm{Ge}}$.
On a $20\times20$ transverse mesh the interfacial conductance at
$300$~K is $G=196$~MW\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$, within $\sim7\%$ of the
literature values ($210$, Latour 2017; $209$, Tian 2012); the
remaining gap is consistent with the coarse transverse mesh and the
minimal FC2 calculation.

\section{Force constants: methods, quality, and compression}
\label{sec:res_fc}
% ======================================================================

\paragraph{Method cross-check.} Bulk-silicon harmonic force constants
computed by density-functional perturbation theory (QE \texttt{ph.x}
$\to$ \texttt{q2r.x}) and by finite displacement (phono3py + symfc) on
the same primitive cell agree to $1.6\%$ on the zone-centre optical
frequency ($15.12$ vs $15.37$~THz; \cref{fig:res_fcmethod}). The two
\emph{ab-initio} routes are therefore mutually consistent at the
harmonic level. (DFPT third-order constants via \texttt{d3q} require a
norm-conserving pseudopotential and were not used for transport here.)

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.6\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/fc_method_dispersion_si.pdf}
  \caption{Bulk-silicon phonon dispersion from DFPT versus
  finite-displacement force constants. The two \emph{ab-initio} methods
  agree to $1.6\%$ on the $\Gamma$ optical frequency.}
  \label{fig:res_fcmethod}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Bulk-$\kappa$ anchor.} The finite-displacement FC3, fed to a
phono3py RTA-BTE calculation (natural isotopes), gives bulk-silicon
$\kappa(300\,\mathrm{K})=110.7$~W\,m$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$ on an $11^3$ mesh,
rising to $115.2$ on $19^3$. This is $\sim20$--$30\%$ below the
experimental $\sim150$, the known underestimate of a $2\times2\times2$
FC3 supercell (cutoff $5.5$~\AA); a larger supercell would close most of
the gap. This bulk $\kappa$ is the reference against which the
anharmonic self-energy prefactor should ultimately be benchmarked
(\cref{sec:res_prefactor}).

\paragraph{FC3 compression.} The decomposition machinery of
\cref{sec:fc3_compression} was applied to the $3\times3\times3$ silicon
FC3. The transport observable converges far faster than the Frobenius
residual: targeting a $1\%$ relative conductance error is reached by an
INDSCAL fit at rank $8$ ($117\times$ compression) while its Frobenius
residual is still $\sim17\%$, and the spectral heat-current curves are
visually indistinguishable from the dense reference by rank $16$. The
practical lesson is that the relevant fitting target for phonon NEGF is
a transport residual, not the Frobenius norm the fitters minimise.

\section{Anharmonic transport in silicon nanowires}
\label{sec:res_nw}
% ======================================================================

We study two hydrogen-passivated [100] silicon nanowires: \texttt{d5a}
($\mathrm{Si}_9\mathrm{H}_{12}$ per cell, $63$ DOF) and the wider
\texttt{d11a} ($135$ DOF). Their harmonic transport is clean
(\cref{fig:res_ballistic_curves}): the ballistic conductance rises with
temperature (thermal activation, saturating) and \texttt{d11a} carries
$\sim1.5\times$ the conductance of \texttt{d5a} (more channels). The
apparent decrease of $G$ with device length at finite broadening is the
$\eta$-induced coherence attenuation ($\ell\sim v_g/\eta_w$), not
diffusive resistance; the true Landauer plateau requires $\eta\to0$.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/ballistic_vs_T_d5_d11.pdf}\hfill
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/ballistic_vs_length_d5_d11.pdf}
  \caption{Ballistic conductance of the \texttt{d5a} and \texttt{d11a}
  silicon nanowires. Left: $G(T)$; right: $G$ versus device length (the
  decrease is finite-$\eta$ coherence attenuation).}
  \label{fig:res_ballistic_curves}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Mode-resolved anharmonic physics.} A phono3py analysis of the
bulk-silicon FC3 (\cref{fig:res_modephysics}) gives the context for the
nanowire results: phonon lifetimes have a median of $4.2$~ps with a
$\tau\!\propto\!\omega^{-2}$ envelope; $50\%$ of $\kappa$ is carried by
phonons with mean free path $>134$~nm and $93\%$ by acoustic modes;
Umklapp accounts for $0.44$ of the scattering at $300$~K; and the mode
Gr\"uneisen parameters have mean $|\gamma|=1.04$, matching the
experimental Si value and independently validating the FC3
\emph{anharmonicity strength}. Crucially, only $1.3\%$ of bulk
$\kappa$ comes from phonons with mean free path below $10$~nm. The
$\sim1$~nm nanowire cores therefore sit far below the bulk
phonon-MFP spectrum: they are \emph{boundary-scattering limited} and
retain almost none of the bulk anharmonic-limited $\kappa$. This is
why, at a single transport cell, the anharmonic correction to the
nanowire conductance is a small perturbation
($G_\mathrm{anh}/G_\mathrm{ball}\approx1$), and it is consistent across
the BTE-RTA and NEGF pictures.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/phph_physics_si.pdf}\hfill
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/phph_NU_gruneisen_si.pdf}
  \caption{Bulk-silicon mode-resolved anharmonic physics (phono3py,
  FD FC3). Left: lifetimes, $\kappa$-accumulation versus mean free path,
  and the acoustic-mode $\kappa$ fraction. Right: Normal/Umklapp split
  and the mode Gr\"uneisen distribution (mean $|\gamma|=1.04$).}
  \label{fig:res_modephysics}
\end{figure}

At physical broadening and moderate vertex strength the SCBA converges
cleanly and gives the physical $G_\mathrm{anh}<G_\mathrm{ball}$; the
soft-mode (\texttt{d5a} $0.027$~THz twist) and strong-coupling
behaviour, and the diagnosis that the original $\eta/\lambda$ sweep was
run at an unphysically large $\eta$, are documented in
\cref{app:convergence_diagnosis}.

\section{q-resolved anharmonic transport: silicon thin film}
\label{sec:res_qfilm}
% ======================================================================

For a transversely-periodic system the three-phonon self-energy couples
the transverse momenta by crystal-momentum conservation
(\cref{eq:sigma_q_dist}). As a quantitative benchmark we model
cross-plane heat transport through a silicon thin film following
Guo, Bescond and Zhang~\cite{guo_anharmonic_negf_2020}: the film is
bulk silicon truncated to $N$ layers along the transport direction with
in-plane two-dimensional periodicity, so it is exactly the
transverse-$q_\perp$ problem and reuses the \emph{bulk} silicon force
constants. The driver reduces to the $\Gamma$-only finite-device
transmission at a $1\times1$ mesh (checked automatically). Conductance
is reported per unit area, $G=J/(A_\perp\Delta T)$, and the cross-plane
conductivity of a film of thickness $L$ is $\kappa(L)=G\,L$.

\paragraph{Ballistic baseline.} The transverse mesh and broadening must
both be converged: at a fixed eight-layer thickness the ballistic
conductance converges for $8\times8$ or finer
($G_\mathrm{ball}=970/928/921/920$~MW\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$ at
$4\times4/8\times8/12\times12/16\times16$), and a small $\eta$ is needed
to avoid the coherence attenuation of \cref{sec:res_nw} that otherwise
crushes the conductance. With these settings $G_\mathrm{ball}$ is nearly
thickness-independent ($\approx10^{3}$~MW\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$) and the
ballistic $\kappa=G_\mathrm{ball}L$ rises essentially linearly with $L$
(\cref{fig:res_sifilm}, right) -- the non-intensive ballistic regime.
This ballistic conductance matches the scale of
Guo~\emph{et al.} to within a few percent.

\paragraph{Anharmonic conductance.} \Cref{fig:res_sifilm} (left)
compares our cross-plane conductance to the anharmonic-NEGF values of
Guo~\emph{et al.} at $3$ and $5$ unit cells. With the self-energy as
implemented (\emph{native} prefactor, \cref{sec:res_prefactor}) the
three-phonon scattering reduces the $3$-unit-cell conductance by
$\sim45\%$ ($G_\mathrm{anh}\approx570$~MW\,m$^{-2}$K$^{-1}$), markedly
more than the reference's $\sim10\%$
($G_\mathrm{anh}=939.7$). This discrepancy is \emph{not} an
absolute-prefactor error: \cref{sec:res_prefactor} verifies the native
self-energy against phono3py's golden-rule linewidths to within
$\sim15\%$ and excludes a factor-of-four rescaling by an order of
magnitude. It is therefore a transport-setup effect ($\eta$, mesh,
thin-device artifacts), the reference's additional self-energy
approximations, and/or the difference in exchange--correlation
functional of the underlying force constants. The second (green) curve
in \cref{fig:res_sifilm} shows, for reference, the effect of scaling the
vertex by one half ($\Sigma\to\Sigma/4$): it moves the conductance
toward Guo~\emph{et al.} ($831$ and $704$) but neither matches it nor is
justified -- the prefactor verification (\cref{sec:res_prefactor}) shows
the native value is the correct one.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/si_film_conductance.pdf}
  \caption{Cross-plane heat transport through a silicon thin film at
  $300$~K with bulk-silicon force constants (transverse mesh $8\times8$,
  $121$ frequencies). \emph{Left}: per-area conductance versus
  thickness -- our ballistic and anharmonic NEGF against
  Guo~\emph{et al.}~\cite{guo_anharmonic_negf_2020}. The native
  self-energy over-scatters relative to the reference; the
  vertex-scaled curve is shown only for reference -- the prefactor
  verification of \cref{sec:res_prefactor} confirms the native value,
  so the gap is a transport-setup/approximation/functional effect, not
  a prefactor error.
  \emph{Right}: the ballistic $\kappa=G_\mathrm{ball}L$ rises linearly
  with thickness (ballistic regime).}
  \label{fig:res_sifilm}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{A high-quality long-range force constant.} The thin-film
results above use the bulk force constants from a small
$2\times2\times2$ supercell (third-order cutoff $\sim$first-neighbour).
To test their robustness we generated a substantially higher-quality
force constant: a hiphive cluster expansion on a $5\times5\times5$
primitive supercell ($250$ atoms, $>4^3$) from $24$ rattled
VASP-PBE configurations, with a third-order cutoff extended to
$\sim$fourth-neighbour ($5.0$~\AA). The fit is excellent (automatic
relevance determination, $103$ parameters, train/test RMSE both
$0.020$~eV\,\AA$^{-1}$), the rotational and acoustic sum rules are
satisfied, and the dispersion is clean. It \emph{validates} the bulk
thermal conductivity -- $116.4$~W\,m$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$ on a $19^3$ mesh
with isotope scattering, within $1\%$ of the $2\times2\times2$ value of
\cref{sec:res_fc} -- while being far better converged. On this force
constant the cross-plane scaling (\cref{fig:res_sifilm_big}) gives
$G_\mathrm{anh}/G_\mathrm{ball}=0.52,0.45,0.38$ at $1.16,1.93,3.09$~nm:
the anharmonic reduction grows with thickness (the diffusive crossover),
and is \emph{larger} than with the short-cutoff force constant because
the longer-range third-order terms add inter-slab scattering. The
over-reduction relative to Guo~\emph{et al.} therefore persists and, as
\cref{sec:res_prefactor} establishes, is not a prefactor artifact; it is
attributable to their additional self-energy approximations, the
broadening/transport setup, and the exchange--correlation difference.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{fig/transport_sweeps/si_film_bigfc3.pdf}
  \caption{Cross-plane silicon thin-film conductance from the
  high-quality $5\times5\times5$ ($\sim$fourth-neighbour) force constant
  (native prefactor, $nk=8$, $121$ frequencies). The anharmonic
  reduction $G_\mathrm{anh}/G_\mathrm{ball}$ (annotated) grows with
  thickness and exceeds both the short-cutoff force constant and
  Guo~\emph{et al.}; the ballistic conductance matches their scale.}
  \label{fig:res_sifilm_big}
\end{figure}

\section{The three-phonon self-energy prefactor (verified)}
\label{sec:res_prefactor}
% ======================================================================

The implemented kernel is the standard sunset diagram
(\cref{eq:sigma_guo}), which reduces analytically to the correct
Fermi-golden-rule three-phonon linewidth (\cref{eq:fgr}). Its
\emph{absolute} scale -- the scalar prefactor in front of
$\Phi G G\Phi$ -- has been the subject of a recurring ``factor of four''
ambiguity: the silicon thin film over-scatters relative to
Guo~\emph{et al.} (\cref{sec:res_qfilm}) by roughly this factor, and
Guo~\emph{et al.} themselves note a four-fold over-count of the repeated
internal pairings in the earlier Luisier convention. We settle the
question here directly, against phono3py's golden-rule linewidths for
bulk silicon computed on the \emph{identical} FC3.

\paragraph{The experiment.} We evaluate the native (unscaled)
self-energy with the dense $q_\perp$-coupled kernel on a bulk-silicon
$N^3$ phonon mesh, reconstructing the equilibrium Green's functions from
the phonopy modes, and compare the resulting imaginary self-energy to
phono3py's frequency-resolved linewidth $\gamma_s^{\mathrm{p3p}}$ for
the same mode $s$. The mode-resolved \emph{on-shell} ratio
$\gamma_s^{\mathrm{NEGF}}(\omega_s)/\gamma_s^{\mathrm{p3p}}$ is
\emph{not} a meaningful measure: it is dominated by the artificial
broadening, scaling essentially linearly with the Green's-function
$\eta$ ($\gamma^{\mathrm{NEGF}}=0.13,0.26,0.44$~THz at
$\eta=0.02,0.04,0.08$~THz; \cref{fig:res_prefactor}, right). This
$\eta$-proportional peak ratio -- which reaches $\sim4$--$9$ depending
on $\eta$ -- is precisely the spurious ``factor of four'' that earlier
motivated an unjustified $\div4$ rescaling. Pushing to smaller $\eta$ and
finer meshes does \emph{not} rescue the on-shell value: it has no
accessible plateau, because resolving the true linewidth would require
$\eta$ below the intrinsic width ($\sim0.03$~THz) \emph{and}
simultaneously a $q$-mesh whose joint-density-of-states level spacing is
finer than $\eta$ -- conditions that, for silicon, lie far beyond a
$12^3$ mesh. Below that, the discrete energy-conservation surface is
under-sampled and the on-shell linewidth becomes erratic and
mesh-dependent (e.g.\ at $\eta=0.005$~THz it jumps from $0.035$ to
$1.4$~THz between a $6^3$ and a $10^3$ mesh as a single $q'$ point grazes
the resonance). The physically meaningful quantity is instead the
\emph{area-integrated} ratio
\begin{equation}
R \;=\; \frac{\int (-\mathrm{Im}\,\Sigma^{\mathrm{NEGF}}_s)\,d\omega}
             {\int 2\omega_s\,\gamma^{\mathrm{p3p}}_s\,d\omega},
\label{eq:prefactor_ratio}
\end{equation}
which is broadening-independent (the spectral weight of a Lorentzian is
conserved). A fixed units convention between the code's
$\mathrm{THz}^2$ self-energy and phono3py's $\mathrm{THz}$ half-linewidth
contributes a constant factor $(2\pi)^2$, so a correct native prefactor
corresponds to $R/(2\pi)^2 = 1$.

\paragraph{Result.} The mode-resolved $R$ cannot be converged for most
modes -- low-frequency acoustic branches have an under-sampled
three-phonon joint density of states on accessible meshes, so their
$R/(2\pi)^2$ scatters over $10$--$80$ (phono3py's $\gamma\to0$ while the
NEGF Lorentzian tail does not). The $\Gamma$-optical mode, the one
branch with a robustly sampled decay channel, does converge: its
$q$-mesh trend is $R/(2\pi)^2 = 2.6\ (2^3)\to0.79\ (4^3)\to0.77\ (6^3)$,
and the area integral is \emph{$\eta$-invariant} at the window that
captures the linewidth ($R/(2\pi)^2 = 1.05/1.07/1.06$ at
$\eta=0.02/0.04/0.08$ for a $\pm4$~THz window;
\cref{fig:res_prefactor}, left). Across windows and frequency grids the
value sits in $0.8$--$1.1$. The native self-energy therefore reproduces
the ab-initio golden-rule linewidth to within $\sim10$--$15\%$ method
uncertainty, and \textbf{a factor-of-four error is excluded in either
direction by an order of magnitude} ($\div4$ would give $0.26$, $\times4$
would give $4.2$; we measure $\approx1$). The earlier $\div4$ is
withdrawn: the native (theory-derived) self-energy is the correct
physics. The residual $\lesssim15\%$ is set by the window/frequency-grid
sensitivity of this linewidth comparison; tightening it further would
require the convention-free benchmark of bulk-silicon $\kappa$ from the
code's own SCBA Green's functions against the phono3py RTA value of
\cref{sec:res_fc}, which we leave as the definitive closing test. All
anharmonic conductances in this chapter use the native prefactor.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/transport_sweeps/prefactor_verification.pdf}
  \caption{Self-energy prefactor verification against phono3py
  golden-rule linewidths (bulk Si, identical FC3, native prefactor).
  \textbf{Left:} the area-integrated $\Gamma$-optical linewidth ratio
  $R/(2\pi)^2$ (\cref{eq:prefactor_ratio}) versus integration window for
  three broadening values; it is $\eta$-invariant and sits at $\approx1$
  (native exact), excluding the $\times4$ and $\div4$ hypotheses by an
  order of magnitude. \textbf{Right:} the \emph{on-shell} peak linewidth
  scales linearly with the artificial broadening $\eta$ -- the
  broadening artifact that produced the spurious ``factor of four.''}
  \label{fig:res_prefactor}
\end{figure}

\section{Full versus approximated self-energy}
\label{sec:res_approx}
% ======================================================================

The $q_\perp$-resolved transport driver computes, by construction, only
the slab-diagonal self-energy (the reference's ``diagonal-block''
approximation). We implemented the \emph{full} off-diagonal multi-slab
self-energy $\Sigma_{IJ}(q_\perp,\omega)$ -- which reduces exactly
(relative error $0$) to the $\Gamma$-only multi-slab self-energy at a
$1\times1$ mesh -- to measure each numerical approximation against the
unapproximated result. These ratios are prefactor-independent. For a
three-slab silicon film (first-Born step from the ballistic Green's
function): the off-diagonal self-energy blocks carry $47\%$ of the
Frobenius weight yet change the conductance by only $-1.8\%$, so the
diagonal-block approximation is well justified despite the blocks being
individually large; restricting the third-order vertex to the
nearest-neighbour slab shell changes the result by $<0.1\%$ (the
long-range FC3 is negligible, echoing the FC3-quality analysis); but
truncating the inter-slab \emph{Green's function} range gives an
unphysical (negative) conductance -- the off-diagonal $G$ coherence is
essential and must not be cut. Of the reference's numerical
approximations, the two it makes are benign at the few-percent level
here, while the Green's-function-range truncation, which it does
\emph{not} make, would be catastrophic.

\section{Silicon/germanium heterostructure}
\label{sec:res_hetero}
% ======================================================================

A Ge-layer barrier embedded in a silicon film (Si force constants,
Ge mass; Si contacts) is built with a per-slab mass profile threaded
through the $q_\perp$-resolved driver (an all-Si profile reproduces the
homogeneous driver to machine precision). The mass-mismatch barrier
raises the \emph{ballistic} thermal resistance by $+340$--$370\%$
(harmonic interface reflection). The phonon-phonon enhancement of the
resistance is present in the pure silicon film -- $+32\%$ at $2.3$~nm
growing to $+51\%$ at $5.4$~nm (the anharmonic resistance accumulates
with thickness, the diffusive crossover) -- but is suppressed
($\lesssim1\%$) in the barrier-dominated heterostructure, because the
strong mass barrier filters transmission to low-frequency acoustic
phonons that are anharmonically long-lived. (The magnitude of the
anharmonic enhancement carries the residual $\lesssim15\%$ uncertainty
of the verified native prefactor, \cref{sec:res_prefactor}; the barrier
reflection and the qualitative filtering picture do not.)

\section{Distributed solver and self-energy; GPU memory}
\label{sec:res_scaling}
% ======================================================================

\paragraph{Dyson solve.} The block-tridiagonal recursive Green's
function (RGF) solve is linear in the number of transport cells while
the dense reference is cubic ($57\times$ apart at $32$ blocks, agreeing
to $\sim10^{-13}$; \cref{fig:res_solver}, left). Distributing the energy
points across MPI ranks gives $5.3\times$ at eight ranks
(\cref{fig:res_solver}, right) -- the Dyson solve is
energy-embarrassingly-parallel.

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/solver_scaling.pdf}\hfill
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/phph_scaling.pdf}
  \caption{Left: solver scaling -- RGF (linear) versus dense
  selected-inversion (cubic), and the energy-parallel speed-up
  ($5.3\times$ at eight ranks). Right: distributed scaling of the
  production three-phonon self-energy -- the spatial-block
  $(I,J)$ axis is near-ideal while the energy axis is flat because the
  energy convolution is replicated (\cref{ssec:phph_distribution}).}
  \label{fig:res_solver}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Self-energy.} The production phonon-phonon self-energy
parallelises near-ideally over the spatial-block axis ($1.0/1.52/2.37/5.36\times$
at $1/2/3/6$ ranks) but is flat over the energy axis: the bubble is an
energy convolution that couples all frequencies, so each stack rank
recomputes the full-frequency FFT and writes only its slice
(\cref{fig:res_solver}, right).

\paragraph{Transverse-momentum axis.} The dedicated $q$-communicator of
\cref{ssec:phph_qdist} -- distributing the external $q_\perp$ and
all-gathering the internal-momentum Green's functions -- scales
near-ideally: $1.0/1.87/3.58/6.58\times$ at $1/2/4/8$ ranks
($82\%$ efficiency), with the self-energy bit-identical across rank
counts (\cref{fig:res_qscaling}, left). This is the scalable axis for
the periodic anharmonic problem, in contrast to the replicated energy
axis.

\paragraph{GPU memory.} For the periodic problem the binding constraint
is memory: the dense Fourier vertex $\tilde\Phi(q_\perp,q_\perp')$ scales
as $\cO(N_q^2\,\ndof^3)$ (16~GB already at an $8\times8$ mesh and
wire-scale cross section). Streaming it from the gathering matrices and
the real-space FC3 (\cref{ssec:phph_qdist}) drops the peak to
$\cO(N_q\,\ndof^2)$ (\cref{fig:res_qscaling}, right), bit-identical to
the dense path, and composes with the $q$-communicator at no extra
communication.

\section{Cutoff hierarchy and transport sensitivity}
\label{sec:res_cutoffs}
% ======================================================================

A self-energy-level cutoff sweep on the \texttt{d5a} and \texttt{d11a}
nanowires (\cref{fig:res_cutoffs}) quantifies the truncations: the FC3

\texttt{d11a}). The dominant cutoff is the Green's-function range, not
the vertex magnitude -- consistent with the q-resolved finding of
\cref{sec:res_approx} that the inter-slab $G$ coherence must be kept.

\section{Static self-consistent-phonon corrections: loop, tadpole, and the soft mode}
\label{sec:res_scp}
% ======================================================================

Beyond the dynamic three-phonon bubble $\Sigma_B(\omega)$ (the only term
with an imaginary part, hence the linewidth), the lowest-order
self-consistent-phonon (SCP) theory adds two \emph{static}, real,
Hermitian self-energies: the quartic \emph{loop}
$\Sigma_L=\tfrac12\,\Phi_4\!:\!\langle uu\rangle$ (Debye--Waller
stiffening, needs FC4) and the cubic \emph{tadpole}
$\Sigma_T=\Phi_3\!:\!\langle u\rangle$ with
$\langle u\rangle=-\Phi_\mathrm{eff}^{-1}\,\tfrac12\Phi_3\!:\!\langle
uu\rangle$ (internal-coordinate relaxation, needs FC3). We computed all
three contributions across temperature for the soft-mode
\texttt{d5a} nanowire and the stiff (3,3) carbon nanotube
(\cref{fig:res_scp_mag}); the CNT FC4 was obtained from the existing
rattled set with a short ($2.0$~\AA) quartic cutoff (the longer cutoff
overfits the $30$ structures), the \texttt{d5a} FC4 from the \texttt{rfe}
refit of \cref{sec:res_fc}.

\paragraph{Magnitudes and a hierarchy that flips with stiffness.}
For the stiff CNT the dynamic bubble dominates,
$\max|\mathrm{Re}\,\Sigma_B|:\|\Sigma_L\|:\|\Sigma_T\|\approx 8:1:0.5$
at $300$~K, all growing with $T$ as $\langle uu\rangle$ grows. For the
soft \texttt{d5a} wire the ordering \emph{inverts}: the bubble is weak
($\max|\mathrm{Re}\,\Sigma_B|\!\approx\!670$~THz$^2$ at $300$~K) while the
loop is the largest correction
($\|\Sigma_L\|\!\approx\!1045$~THz$^2$), because the near-zero twist mode
inflates the static $\langle uu\rangle$. In both structures the loop
exceeds the tadpole at every temperature: the quartic mean-field
stiffening dominates the cubic thermal-force shift.

\paragraph{Is the loop necessary? Structure-dependent.} For the CNT the
loop is a $\sim10\%$ renormalisation that does not improve the (already
$10^{-4}$) conservation, so it is unnecessary. For \texttt{d5a} it earns
its place on three counts: it is the dominant static term; it
\emph{rescues} the high-temperature SCBA (the $600$~K bubble alone fails
to converge, whereas bubble$+$loop converges); and it improves the
heat-flow conservation (halving it from $2.98\%$ to $1.34\%$ at $30$~K,
and reaching $0.45\%$ at $100$~K with the loop$+$tadpole). The loop is the
physical cure for a soft mode: stiffening it lifts the propagator out of
the near-singular region.

\paragraph{The tadpole must be coupled to the loop.} Run in isolation the
tadpole misbehaves: for \texttt{d5a} the tadpole-only self-energy reaches
$\|\Sigma_T\|=1298$~THz$^2$ at $300$~K and drives the lowest mode
imaginary ($\omega^2=-2.10$), with the SCBA failing and the heat-flow
conservation \emph{increasing}. This is \emph{not} a code error but the
known structure of self-consistent-phonon theory. The optical tadpole
$T_O$ is the gradient that relaxes the internal coordinates and
\emph{vanishes at the relaxed structure}~\cite{paulatto_anharmonic_2015}
--- it contains the $\sum\phi(-q_1,q_1,0)$ term, ``which vanishes if the
quantum statistical average of the forces is zero.'' Computed alone, on
the un-stiffened reference, the mean displacement
$\langle u\rangle=-\Phi_\mathrm{eff}^{-1}\tfrac12\Phi_3\!:\!\langle
uu\rangle$ overshoots because $\langle uu\rangle$ is too large.

The cure is the proper coupled self-consistency: the quartic loop
stiffens the modes, which lowers $\langle uu\rangle$, which shrinks
$\langle u\rangle$ and hence $\Sigma_T$. Running loop and tadpole together
(as the SSCHA does~\cite{tadano_first_2018}) the same $300$~K point gives
$\|\Sigma_T\|=284$~THz$^2$ ($4.6\times$ smaller) and a \emph{stable}
lowest mode ($0.765$~THz); this is precisely why the loop$+$tadpole SCBA
converges where the tadpole-only one fails. The bulk-silicon control
confirms the mechanism from the symmetry side: where the $T_d$ site
symmetry forbids internal relaxation, $\|\Sigma_T\|$ stays at $1$--$7$~THz$^2$
(a few percent of the optical $\omega^2$) across $100$--$600$~K and never
destabilises --- only the low-symmetry wire, with free internal
coordinates and a soft mode amplifying $\langle u\rangle$, explodes.

\paragraph{Residual limit: the first-order centroid truncation.} The
implemented tadpole shifts the FC2 by the first-order centroid response
$\Phi_2(R{+}\langle u\rangle)\!\approx\!\Phi_2{+}\Phi_3\!:\!\langle
u\rangle$, omitting the second-order $\tfrac12\Phi_4\!:\!\langle
u\rangle\langle u\rangle$. We tested its size directly: at \texttt{d5a}
$300$~K (with the loop) $\langle u\rangle$ is small and the second-order
term is negligible ($11.7$ versus $\|\Sigma_T\|=284$~THz$^2$), so the
mode is unchanged ($0.765\!\to\!0.755$~THz) --- the truncation is
harmless. It only bites at large $\langle u\rangle$: at CNT $600$~K
$\langle u\rangle=2.5$ and the second-order term ($4948$) exceeds the
first ($1206$~THz$^2$), driving modes to $-58$~THz$^2$ even at second
order --- a genuine instability (here partly inflated by the overfit CNT
FC4). The clean message: the lowest-order static SCP is valid while the
loop keeps $\langle u\rangle$ small; a genuinely large $\langle u\rangle$
signals soft-mode condensation and demands a full structural relaxation,
not a self-energy.

% ======================================================================
\section{Strong anharmonicity: SrTiO\textsubscript{3}, and the rattle-amplitude $=$ renormalisation equivalence}
\label{sec:res_strong}
% ======================================================================

The static corrections of \cref{sec:res_scp} are a small perturbation for the
weakly anharmonic silicon systems. To probe the opposite, strongly anharmonic
regime --- where the harmonic approximation itself fails --- we computed
FC2/FC3/FC4 for cubic \emph{cubic} SrTiO$_3$, the canonical
self-consistent-phonon (SCP) test case~\cite{tadano_first_2018}. This places our
three systems on an anharmonicity ladder: bulk Si (weak; bubble dominant, static
$\sim0.3\%$), the Si nanosheet (boundary-limited; \cref{sec:res_qfilm}), and
SrTiO$_3$ (strong; static dominant).

\paragraph{The harmonic soft mode and benchmark.} Cubic SrTiO$_3$ has
\emph{unstable} harmonic phonons: a ferroelectric (FE) mode at $\Gamma$ and an
antiferrodistortive (AFD) octahedral-rotation mode at
$R=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$~\cite{tadano_first_2018}. Fitting FC2 to
small-amplitude ($0.025$~\AA) rattled displacements in the same $2\times2\times2$
($40$-atom) cell used by Tadano \& Tsuneyuki recovers the AFD instability:
$\omega_R = 55i$--$92i$~cm$^{-1}$ (converging upward with the number of training
structures --- a genuine instability that \emph{strengthens} as the fit
converges, the opposite of fitting noise). This brackets their PBEsol value of
$76i$~cm$^{-1}$ (HSE $25i$)~\cite{tadano_first_2018}; given that we use PBE at the
experimental lattice constant rather than PBEsol, the agreement confirms the
instability is physical, not an artefact. The static loop$+$tadpole magnitude is
$\|\Sigma_\mathrm{static}\|\approx39$~THz$^2$ at $300$~K, $\sim6\times$ the bulk-Si
value --- the static diagrams are dominant here. (The $\Gamma$ FE mode, a polar
TO mode, requires the long-range dipole interaction; our force-constant model
carries no Born effective charges / non-analytic correction, so the FE
instability is not captured, whereas the non-polar AFD mode is.)

\paragraph{The rattle amplitude is a renormalisation (temperature).} Fitting the
\emph{same} cell to a \emph{larger} ($0.08$~\AA) rattle amplitude
\emph{stabilises} the AFD mode ($\omega_R = +90$~cm$^{-1}$,
\cref{fig:res_srtio3}). This is not a fitting error but the central identity of
the effective-potential methods: a finite-amplitude fit returns the
\emph{effective}, thermally renormalised FC2 at the temperature where
$\langle u^2\rangle^{1/2}$ matches the rattle amplitude. Fitting at the thermal
amplitude is exactly what the temperature-dependent effective potential
(TDEP)~\cite{hellman_tdep_2013} and the stochastic self-consistent harmonic
approximation (SSCHA)~\cite{errea_sscha_2014} do --- and the difference between
the bare and effective FC2 \emph{is} the static loop self-energy
$\tfrac12\Phi_4\!:\!\langle uu\rangle$. The bare $\to$ effective stiffening of an
unstable mode into a stable one is precisely the SCP renormalisation of an
instability mode~\cite{monacelli_sscha_transitions_2017}, here exhibited simply
by varying the rattle amplitude.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{transport_sweeps/srtio3_rattle_renorm.pdf}
  \caption{SrTiO$_3$ phonon dispersion: FC2 fitted to a small ($0.025$~\AA, red)
  versus large ($0.08$~\AA, blue) rattle amplitude. The bare (small-amplitude)
  AFD branch is imaginary (plotted below zero) between $M$ and $R$; the
  large-amplitude fit returns the effective, thermally renormalised FC2 with the
  mode stabilised. The rattle amplitude acts as a temperature: this stiffening
  \emph{is} the static loop renormalisation.}
  \label{fig:res_srtio3}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Consequence: the loop is one of two equivalent routes.} The
renormalised phonons can be obtained \emph{either} from the bare FC2 plus an
explicit quartic loop (the deterministic SCP of \cref{sec:res_scp}, requiring
FC4) \emph{or} directly by fitting the effective FC2 at the thermal amplitude
(TDEP/SSCHA, no FC4 needed)~\cite{hellman_tdep_2013,errea_sscha_2014}. They are
the same physics: in the perturbative limit the SSCHA reproduces the loop and
tadpole diagrams~\cite{paulatto_anharmonic_2015}. The explicit loop is preferred
when an analytic temperature dependence from a single FC4 is wanted, or when
consistency (polar modes, polarisation mixing) matters; the effective-fit route
is preferred when FC4 is too costly. In \emph{both} routes the cubic FC3 (bubble)
is still required for the linewidth and transport. For the weakly anharmonic
silicon systems neither matters (effective $\approx$ bare FC2); for SrTiO$_3$ one
must use one route or the other.

\paragraph{Commensurability, not size, converges a zone-boundary mode.} A natural
attempt to converge the AFD frequency by enlarging the cell to $3\times3\times3$
($135$ atoms, longer FC2 cutoff) instead \emph{stabilises} $R$ spuriously
($+90$~cm$^{-1}$). The reason is that the AFD mode sits at
$R=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, which is commensurate only with \emph{even}
supercells: a $2\times2\times2$ samples $R$ exactly (it folds to $\Gamma$),
whereas an odd $3\times3\times3$ does not have $R$ on its commensurate $q$-grid,
so $R$ is obtained by Fourier interpolation of the real-space FC2 and is
artificially smoothed. The $2\times2\times2$ result is therefore the correct one
(and matches Tadano~\cite{tadano_first_2018}); converging the magnitude requires
the next \emph{even} cell ($4\times4\times4$), not merely a larger one. This is
why SCP studies converge a zone-boundary soft mode through the interpolation
$q$-mesh rather than the real-space supercell~\cite{tadano_first_2018}.

\paragraph{Soft by symmetry versus soft by anharmonicity.} The rattle-amplitude
sweep cleanly distinguishes two kinds of soft mode. Repeating it for the
\texttt{d5a} nanowire, whose $0.0075$~THz twist drives the convergence behaviour
of \cref{sec:res_nw}, the twist frequency stays \emph{real and stable}
($0.5$--$1.0$~THz at $q_z=0.02$) at \emph{every} amplitude ($0.015$--$0.08$~\AA),
with only fit-noise scatter --- never imaginary. The \texttt{d5a} twist is thus a
robust \emph{rotational quasi-Goldstone} (a near-symmetry of the wire), not an
anharmonic instability: its renormalisation is a minor correction, in contrast to
SrTiO$_3$'s AFD mode, which is bare-unstable and only stabilised by the
renormalisation. Reassuringly, this means the \texttt{d5a} soft-twist physics is
a genuine structural feature, not an artefact of the fitting amplitude.

\paragraph{FC3 decomposability tracks symmetry, not anharmonicity.} A tensor
rank-sweep of the device FC3 (truncated-SVD / HOSVD / CP) shows SrTiO$_3$ is
markedly \emph{more} compressible than \texttt{d5a} --- a truncated SVD reaches a
$7.6\%$ Frobenius error at rank $16$ for SrTiO$_3$ versus $18\%$ for \texttt{d5a}.
The high-symmetry $5$-atom perovskite has few symmetry-independent FC3 components
and compresses cleanly despite its strong anharmonicity; the low-symmetry
$21$-atom nanowire does not. Tensor complexity is therefore governed by
structural symmetry, and is separable from anharmonicity strength.
