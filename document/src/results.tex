\section{Ballistic Phonon NEGF}
\label{sec:harmonic_validation}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------

To validate the harmonic transport implementation, the QuaTrEx results
are compared against simpler reference calculations based on direct
Sancho--Rubio decimation and the Caroli formula.  A naive
Sancho--Rubio + Caroli Python code is used as reference.  These
reference cases serve both as correctness checks for the harmonic
backbone and as a baseline for interpreting later anharmonic
corrections. We also compare to Si/Ge transmission results in \citet{guo_anharmonic_2021}.

\subsection{Bulk validation}
\label{sec:bulk_validation}

The simplest test is a homogeneous bulk silicon system.  In this case,
the extracted harmonic blocks are used both in QuaTrEx and in a
standalone reference implementation of Sancho--Rubio plus Caroli.  Both
calculations are run with identical harmonic inputs, the same
transverse-wavevector mesh, and the same frequency grid.

Agreement between the two transmission curves confirms that:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the block extraction from phonopy is correct,
  \item the BTD assembly from unit-cell blocks is consistent,
  \item the open-boundary-condition treatment in QuaTrEx matches the
        reference implementation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Si/Ge interface}
\label{sec:sige}

\begin{figure}[htb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig/reference/si_ge_quatrex_comparison.png}
  \caption{Ballistic phonon transmission on a $20\times20$ transverse
    $k$-mesh with 101 frequency points.
    \textbf{Left:} Homogeneous bulk Si.
    \textbf{Right:} Si/Ge interface in the mass-mismatch approximation.}
  \label{fig:quatrex_comparison}
\end{figure}

As a more realistic reference problem, a ballistic Si/Ge interface is
constructed using a mass-mismatch model.  In this approximation, the
harmonic force constants are taken from bulk Si throughout, and the
distinction between Si and Ge enters only through the atomic masses,
\begin{equation}
  m_\mathrm{Si} = 28.09\;\mathrm{amu},
  \qquad
  m_\mathrm{Ge} = 72.64\;\mathrm{amu}.
\end{equation}
This is justified by the similarity of the Si and Ge interatomic force
constants together with their substantial mass difference.

The heterogeneous system is partitioned into three regions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Left lead:} Si force constants with Si masses,
  \item \textbf{Device:} Si force constants with mixed Si/Ge masses,
  \item \textbf{Right lead:} Si force constants with Ge masses.
\end{itemize}
Atoms with fractional coordinate $z<0.5$ are
assigned Si mass in the device cell and the remainder Ge mass.  The
coupling between left lead and device is taken from the Si lead, while
the coupling between device and right lead is taken from the Ge lead.

For the harmonic inputs, the Si force constants are obtained from a DFT
calculation at the equilibrium lattice constant $a = \qty{5.466}{\angstrom}$.
The calculation uses the conventional 8-atom diamond cell and a
$2\times2\times2$ supercell. The DFT parameters are summarized in
\cref{tab:si-dft-parameters}. Because of diamond symmetry, this setup
reduces to a single displaced calculation in the harmonic
finite-displacement workflow.

interface. On loading, the lattice vectors and harmonic force constants

\unit{\electronvolt\per\angstrom\squared}, respectively, as described
in \cref{sec:phonopy_units}. The Ge and interface models are obtained
by cloning the Si harmonic model and substituting the atomic masses.

At the $\Gamma$ point, the maximum optical frequency of the Si model is
15.55\,THz, consistent with reference values.  The Ge mass-mismatch
clone gives 9.67\,THz, in agreement with the simple mass-scaling
relation
\begin{equation}
  f_\mathrm{Ge}
  = f_\mathrm{Si}\sqrt{\frac{m_\mathrm{Si}}{m_\mathrm{Ge}}}.
\end{equation}

The resulting interfacial thermal conductance at 300\,K is
\begin{equation}
  G = 196\;\mathrm{MW/(m^2\,K)}.
\end{equation}
This may be compared with the literature values

  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The remaining $\sim 7\%$ deviation is likely due to the coarser
transverse-wavevector mesh and the very coarse DFT and FC2 calculation.

\subsection{Numerical comparison on silicon FC3}
\label{sub:fc3_results}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------

We fit the four currently working compression approaches of \cref{sub:fc3_matsvd,sub:fc3_tucker,sub:fc3_cp,sub:fc3_indscal}
to the mass-weighted FC3 tensor
$\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{(3 \nprim)\times \ndof \times \ndof}$ produced by finite-displacement
DFT in a $3{\times}3{\times}3$ supercell of silicon. The working are
$\nprim=2$, $\ndof=3\nprim \Ncell^3 = 162$, so the dense tensor has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fc3_full_entries}
  N_{\mathrm{full}}
  = \nprim\cdot 3\cdot (\ndof)^{2}
  = 6\cdot 162^{2}
  = 157\,464
\end{equation}
double-precision entries.

All transport numbers are obtained by feeding the reconstructed $\tilde\Phi$ back into the same
SCBA + NEGF pipeline used for the dense reference: a single device slab, a $4{\times}4$
transverse $\qp$-mesh, $400$ positive frequency bins in $[0,22]$~THz, $T=300$~K and
$\Delta T=10$~K, Anderson mixing of depth~$5$, SCBA tolerance $10^{-10}$, and at most $500$
SCBA iterations. We compute the anharmonic thermal conducatance
$G_{\mathrm{anh}}$ per area (MW\,m$^{-2}$\,K$^{-1}$).
Errors on both axes are relative to the dense reference, computed once with the raw FC3.

\paragraph{Fit protocol.} All fits are measured by the relative Frobenius error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fc3_rel_err}
  \varepsilon(\tilde\Phi)
  = \frac{\|\Phi - \tilde\Phi\|_F}{\|\Phi\|_F}
\end{equation}
with respect to the dense tensor. The mSVD and HOSVD are computed in closed form from
LAPACK SVDs of the relevant matricisations. HOSVD is followed by $8$ steps of HOOI refinement.
The CP and INDSCAL fits combine a CP-ALS initialisation (from the \texttt{tensorly} package) with
progressive warm-starts from the previous rank, followed by $400$--$600$ L-BFGS iterations with
a strong-Wolfe line search on the full factor set. $3$--$4$ random restarts are taken per rank
and the best-of-restarts fit is kept. Each ansatz is fitted in two variants: an unconstrained
``plain'' variant and an ``ASR'' variant in which the factor matrices are iteratively projected
onto the acoustic-sum-rule null space during optimisation.

The Waring / symmetric-CP fitter of
\cref{sub:fc3_symcp} does not yet converge reliably on this reference tensor and is therefore
omitted from the numerical results below. The present implementation combines shifted
symmetric higher-order power-method (SS-HOPM) initialisation \cite{kolda_shifted_2011} with an
L-BFGS refinement of a single shared factor matrix. On ranks $R\ge 16$ we observe the
classical symptoms of the non-closed rank-$R$ set \cite{stegeman_degeneracy_2006,
de_silva_tensor_2008}: the loss decreases slowly while the factor norms $\|v_r\|$ grow
unboundedly and near-cancelling rank-one terms appear, so that stopping at any finite
iteration count gives a residual that depends sensitively on the random seed and is often
worse than the INDSCAL fit at the same rank. Norm penalties, trust-region L-BFGS, and
degeneracy-aware line searches \cite{stegeman_degeneracy_2006, kolda_numerical_2015} were trued
but did not work to improve the sweep.

\paragraph{Frobenius convergence.} \Cref{fig:fc3_frob_and_ganh}~(a) plots $\varepsilon$ versus
parameter count for the four working compressions. The transport axis in
\cref{fig:fc3_frob_and_ganh}~(b) and the left panel of \cref{fig:fc3_kappa_and_spectral}
converge dramatically faster than the Frobenius residual. Already at rank $R=8$ the relative
conductance error $\varepsilon_G = |G - G_{\mathrm{dense}}|/G_{\mathrm{dense}}$ is
$0.78\%$ (mSVD, $\varepsilon=12.8\%$), $0.71\%$ (CP, $\varepsilon=16.4\%$) and $0.33\%$
(INDSCAL, $\varepsilon=16.6\%$). By $R=16$--$32$ all methods sit below $0.5\%$ in transport
while their Frobenius residuals are still in the range $5$--$11\%$.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/fc3_prim_vasp/frob_and_ganh_vs_params.pdf}
  \caption{FC3 compression on the $3{\times}3{\times}3$ silicon primitive-row tensor
    ($\ndof=162$).
    (a)~Relative Frobenius error \eqref{eq:fc3_rel_err} versus parameter count on log--log
    axes. (b)~Absolute anharmonic conductance $G_{\mathrm{anh}}$ (MW\,m$^{-2}$\,K$^{-1}$)
    versus parameter count on a semi-log $x$ axis. The dashed horizontal line marks the dense
    reference $G_{\mathrm{anh}}^{\mathrm{dense}}=757.46$~MW\,m$^{-2}$\,K$^{-1}$. The dotted vertical line marks $N_{\mathrm{nnz}}$, the
    non-zeros in the full matrix.}
  \label{fig:fc3_frob_and_ganh}
\end{figure}

In particular, the spectral heat current curves in
\cref{fig:fc3_kappa_and_spectral}~(b) are visually indistinguishable from the dense reference
already at INDSCAL $R=16$--$24$ or mSVD $R=16$, while the corresponding Frobenius residuals are
$10\%$ or more. only at $R\le 4$ does the spectral current show visible distortion. The practical consequence is that on this system the relevant quality metric for phonon NEGF is
some observable transport residual $\varepsilon_G$ (or the spectral current residual), not the Frobenius
norm that the fitters themselves minimise. Targeting $\varepsilon_G\le 1\%$ is already achieved
by INDSCAL at $R=8$ ($1\,344$ parameters, $117\times$ compression) and mSVD at $R=8$ ($9\,080$
parameters, $17\times$ compression). Reaching $\varepsilon_G\le 0.1\%$ costs INDSCAL $R=32$
($29\times$), HOSVD $(6,32)$ ($19\times$) or mSVD $R=32$ ($4.3\times$).

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/fc3_prim_vasp/kappa_err_and_spectral.pdf}
  \caption{(a)~Relative thermal-conductance error
    $|G-G_{\mathrm{dense}}|/G_{\mathrm{dense}}$ versus parameter count on log--log axes;
    dotted vertical line: $N_{\mathrm{nnz}}$ reference.
    (b)~Spectral heat current $J(\omega)$ for representative fits against the dense reference
    (red) and the purely ballistic current (dotted grey). The compressed curves overlay the
    dense reference visibly at INDSCAL $R\ge 16$ and mSVD $R\ge 16$.}
  \label{fig:fc3_kappa_and_spectral}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Conservation of heat current.} The heat-flow conservation residual
$|J_L-J_R|/(|J_L|+|J_R|)$ remains below $0.2\%$ across the entire sweep. The dense reference
sits at $0.01\%$. All compressed fits stay within a factor $\sim 20$ of that floor,
with the largest deviations --- $1.66\%$ for CP $R=4$ and $1.29\%$ for INDSCAL $R=2$
--- at the very lowest ranks where $\varepsilon$ is $60$--$80\%$. Once $R\ge 8$ the conservation
residual falls back to $0.01$--$0.15\%$ for every method, and shows no systematic trend with
rank. The ASR-projected variants do not improve this floor in any measurable way.

\section{Finite-Structure Validation}
\label{sec:finite_validation}

\paragraph{Motivation.} Before tackling the full $q_\perp$-resolved anharmonic
problem, we validate the harmonic and cubic force-constant inputs and the
resulting phonon-phonon self-energy machinery on a family of \emph{finite}
structures (no transverse periodicity, ``$\Gamma$-only'' devices). Each
system is processed by a uniform analysis driver
(\path{phonon/input_calc/finite_analysis/}) that produces a fixed report of
quality, sparsity, decomposition, physical-invariant, and self-energy
diagnostics. The analyses below are reported for the H-passivated SiNW(100)
of Sec.~\ref{sec:fc3_compression}\todo{cross-ref correct subsection} and a
finite Si chain (8 atoms, the smallest test fixture); additional structures

\subsection{FC3 tensor decompositions on finite structures}
\label{sec:fv_decomp}

The five decomposition methods of \cref{sec:fc3_compression} (PCP is
disabled by default; cf.\ \cref{ap:fa_modules}) are run on the
SiNW(100) supercell FC3 tensor at ranks $R\in\{2,4,8,16\}$.  Two
findings dominate the result and shape the plot design:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Best-rank Frobenius residuals are large}. mSVD reaches
  $\varepsilon = 0.25$ at $R=16$, HOSVD/CP/INDSCAL $\varepsilon
  \approx 0.30$--$0.32$. The 252-DOF supercell tensor is too high-
  dimensional for the ranks reachable here in reasonable time.
\item \emph{The transport observable collapses to ballistic
  ($G_\mathrm{anh} \to G_\mathrm{ball}$) for 15 of 20 fits.}
  When the reconstruction has $\varepsilon \gtrsim 0.6$ the
  reconstructed FC3 is close to zero in Frobenius norm; the
  phonon-phonon self-energy bubble is then negligible, the SCBA
  reaches its fixed point in two iterations at $\Sigma \approx 0$,
  and the device transports ballistically. In the
  $G_\mathrm{anh}$-vs.-parameter plot
  (\cref{fig:fv_transport_quality_sinw}, panel a) we flag these
  fits with an open red marker and draw both the dense
  $G_\mathrm{anh}$ and the dense $G_\mathrm{ball}$ reference lines.
  Only Waring at $R=16$ (and to a lesser extent CP/INDSCAL at the
  same rank) sit between the two — i.e., produce a non-trivial
  bubble at all.
\end{itemize}

The Waring fitter is currently unreliable below $R=8$: at $R\in\{2,4\}$
it returns the zero tensor (Frobenius residual $\approx 1$,
reconstruction nnz $= 0$). These rows are excluded from the
nnz-vs-parameter plot (\cref{fig:fv_decomp_sinw}, panel b) to avoid a
spurious vertical jump from the rank-4 zero to the rank-16
$\sim\!1.5\times 10^{5}$ entries; the failures are listed in the
figure's footnote.

\subsection{Phonon-phonon self-energy sparsity}
\label{sec:fv_sse}

The block-resolved heatmap of $\|\Sigma^<_{IJ}\|_F$
(\cref{fig:fv_sse_sinw}, left) is organised by transport slabs
(16 slabs of 5--6 atoms each for the $d_5$ supercell, in $z$-sorted
order). The visible blockiness — diagonal slabs are systematically
brighter than off-diagonal ones — is a real feature of the
short-range bubble: $\Sigma_{IJ}$ is driven by triplet diagrams
$\Phi^{(I,K,K')}\,G\,G\,\Phi^{(J,K',K)}$ that are non-zero only when
both $\Phi$ vertices share atoms with the on-block atoms, so
$|I-J|=0$ has more contributing triplets than $|I-J|=1$.

To rule out a spurious diagonal-aggregation bug we also report a
per-atom-pair Frobenius heatmap
(\path{sse_sigma_atomic_heatmap_synth.pdf}): reshape each
$(n_\omega,3 n_A,3 n_B)$ block into $(n_\omega,n_A,3,n_B,3)$ and
reduce the Frobenius norm over the $(\omega,3,3)$ axes. In this
atomic-resolution view the bright diagonals continue smoothly across
the slab boundaries (the dashed white grid), confirming that
neighbouring atoms across a slab boundary couple as strongly as
diametrically-opposite atoms within the same slab. The slab-binned
appearance is purely an aggregation artefact: a $(I,I)$ slab pair
sums $\sim n_A^2$ atomic Frobenius contributions whereas
$(I,I\pm 1)$ sums only the $\sim 2 n_A$ atom pairs that lie within
$r_\mathrm{cut}^{(3)}$ of the slab boundary.
