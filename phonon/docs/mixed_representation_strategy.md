# A mixed, structure-independent SCBA representation

## Outcome

A mixed method is plausible, but it should not mean applying every
approximation to every structure.  The reusable object is a **material-blind
decision and error-control layer** around three interchangeable representations:

1. a coarse uniform frequency mesh for smooth weight, augmented by local
   piecewise-polynomial cells and, only where they pass source/rank/cost gates,
   passive rational clusters;
2. the exact spatial self-energy pattern generated by the retained Green
   blocks and finite-device FC3 graph, followed by compression only of a
   directly generated residual tail;
3. a common FC3-action interface selecting validated tensor factors, exact
   atom-sparse contraction, or the dense oracle.

This is more promising than a universal pole model or universal HSS fit.  The
real repo beds already reject both universal choices.  It is also compatible
with a production SCBA solver: the grid/cell/auxiliary coefficients must be one
mixed fixed-point state, and all bubble terms must be projections of one
bilinear discrete functional.  The method must not add a pole correction after
an independently discretized bubble.

The immediate production priority is an exact block-banded selected solve and
an adaptive frequency-cell pilot.  Selective passive pole promotion comes
after those two baselines.  Spatial hierarchical compression is optional and
should be attempted only beyond the certified exact pattern.

The adaptive-frequency pilot now has a decisive follow-up in
[`nonuniform_grid_review.md`](nonuniform_grid_review.md).  Exact direct P1
product integration establishes that a nonuniform collision basis can work,
but its reference contraction is quadratic.  Quatrex's present
energy-adjoint primary/auxiliary bridge cannot eliminate the fine uniform SCBA
grid and is not automatically conserving: two CNT grids fail the converged
lead-balance gate, while mild long-Si coarsening passes with little speedup.
This is why the mixed basis below needs conservative adaptive cells and
selective rational clusters rather than choosing between them.

The exact follow-up in
[`adaptive_collision_followup.md`](adaptive_collision_followup.md) implements
the missing fast dyadic P1 product, including noncommutative FC3 callbacks and
exact zeroth/first collision moments.  It succeeds on isolated subcell lines,
but one shared Si mesh is not economical: moving q-resolved resonances turn a
373-cell mesh into about 4.03 million transformed modes.  The spectral part of
the mixed representation must therefore be a pole-informed, block-sparse
joint `(q, frequency)` hierarchy with q-local patches; taking the union as a
single global nonuniform grid is now a measured no-go.

The MoS2 follow-up also found an implementation-level reason to make the
frequency abstraction explicit.  Its production primary grid is nonuniform
(262 points, cell widths 0.0200--0.1328 THz), while the current pole kernel's
cell-integrated continuation requires a uniform grid.  The interacting census
therefore refuses that primary grid rather than returning a misleading pole
set.  MoS2 already supplies a uniform auxiliary bubble grid (`dw=0.02 THz`,
`fmax=32 THz`); a future mixed basis should locate/project clusters on its own
uniform or local cells instead of assuming the user-visible primary nodes are
uniform.  The recorded uniform-primary MoS2 pole run is labelled as a
two-iteration linewidth proxy, not production evidence.

That proxy (Alps job `4553188`, `h=0.1 THz`) accepted 300 physical poles over
all 25 q points; bosonic partner closure stores 600 poles in 255 clusters.
Its `gamma/h` minimum, median, and 90th percentile are `2.34e-13`, `0.00334`,
and `0.137`.  These near-real values cannot be trusted as converged lifetimes,
but they do prove that a uniform coarse grid would not resolve the first
interacting iterate.  More importantly, only 5.88 % of stored cluster windows
have measured Keldysh-source variation at or below 0.1.  The affected pole-cell
pairs carry only 0.678 % of integrated ring weight, though as much as 11.9 % at
one frequency.  This combination argues for FC3/bubble-weighted selection:
promote a small, locally important subset with a multi-anchor positive source
and give the rest adaptive cells.  Wholesale promotion would spend state rank
on globally weak, badly modelled poles.

For Si, the real pole census contains 365 clusters and 1184 poles on an
`h=0.125 THz` grid.  `gamma/h` has minimum 0.0178, tenth percentile 0.0934,
and median 0.353.  Yet the complete constant-source auxiliary fit has 15.9 %
median error, reaches physical rank 48/48, and its worst-q Woodbury cost model
is 44.5 times the two-cell reblock.  Narrowness alone is therefore not a
promotion criterion.

The archived source-variation diagnostic is even stricter than the constant-
source leg error: none of the stored Si L8 cluster windows is at or below 0.1.
The two measures are not numerically interchangeable, but both reject one
constant source per complete cluster and motivate positive multi-anchor source
models or local-cell fallback.

## One frequency functional with three basis types

Write every lesser/greater Green function in one coefficient vector

\[
  G^{x}(\omega)=R_C(\omega)c^x+R_A(\omega)a^x+
  \sum_s U_s(\omega I-K_s)^{-1}Q_s^x
  (\omega I-K_s)^{-H}U_s^\dagger,
  \qquad x\in\{<,>\}.
\]

`C` is a coarse, uniform, piecewise-linear grid.  `A` is a nested collection
of compactly supported refinement hats.  `K_s,Q_s` are coherent passive
clusters, with positive-semidefinite Keldysh sources `Q_s`.  The first
implementation should use nonnegative P1/Bernstein reconstruction, not an
unconstrained high-order polynomial, so a convex combination of positive
nodal spectral matrices stays positive.  Its Keldysh projection should use
positive mass-lumped/product-integration weights or an explicit half-diagram
factor; a consistent but sign-indefinite modal projection is not enough.

There must be one projected bubble

\[
  \sigma^x_\alpha =
  \sum_{\beta\gamma}
  W_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,\mathcal V
  \bigl(g^x_\beta,g^x_\gamma\bigr),
\]

where `alpha,beta,gamma` range over all three basis types and `V` is the
existing tensor-decomposed or sparse FC3 action.  Reconstruction `R` and
projection `P` must be adjoints in the chosen frequency inner product.  This
makes the discretization one approximate Phi functional rather than a sum of
incompatible quadratures.

The mixed collision engine can retain the fast paths:

* `C-C`: the existing uniform-grid FFT convolution;
* any term containing `A`: precomputed sparse triple-product/connection
  weights, because adaptive hats have compact support;
* `C-P` and `P-C`: product-integration weights forming Toeplitz kernels, batched
  with FFTs;
* `P-P`: analytic state-space convolution using Kronecker-sum generators;
* narrow sum/difference outputs: remain auxiliary states until their projected
  weight is demonstrably smooth, rather than being sampled immediately onto
  the coarse mesh.

This combination has precedents, although no cited method is a drop-in phonon
Keldysh SCBA solver.  Conservative collision discretizations use a weak-form
piecewise-polynomial basis and retain a quadratic collision form
([Zhang and Gamba](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1104792)); conservative
Fourier--Galerkin schemes show how fast convolution and moment constraints can
coexist ([Pareschi and Rey](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1423452)).  Adaptive
multi-resolution operator algorithms show how a fixed nested basis supports
error-controlled local enrichment
([Beylkin, Cheruvu, and Perez](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.0747)).  Auxiliary
open systems demonstrate controlled steady-state bath representations in
nonequilibrium many-body theory
([Dorda et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.89.165105)), while
passivity-preserving model reduction explains why one should reduce a spectral
factor rather than an unconstrained response
([Breiten and Unger](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2022.110368)).

### Promotion and refinement policy

At the beginning of an SCBA epoch:

1. probe a nested fine set and compute a vector-valued residual for spectral
   weight, Keldysh source variation, and FC3-weighted bubble contribution;
2. keep smooth intervals on `C`, add local hats `A` where an interval error is
   large, and only propose a rational cluster if its linewidth is subcell;
3. promote the cluster only if a positive multi-anchor source fit meets the
   requested bubble tolerance, its output combination rank stays below the
   measured break-even rank, and its FC3-weighted contribution is material;
4. otherwise refine its local cells.  There is always a non-rational fallback;
5. freeze the selected basis while Anderson mixing converges.  Between epochs,
   transfer the state with the adjoint projection and restart mixer history.

The first gate values should be conservative: source/bubble proxy below
`2e-3`, normalized symmetry/positivity defect below `1e-10`, and an estimated
auxiliary apply/solve cost below the exact spatial baseline.  They are
acceptance gates, not universal physical constants.

This policy explains the observed structures.  CNT never enters the rational
branch.  Si enters it for only a selected subset; rejected overlapping clusters
receive local cells.  MoS2 currently exits at the reference-certification gate.

## Exact spatial graph before compression

Let `p` be the actual primitive-cell reach of the stored finite-device FC3
vertices and let `b` be the retained Green-block range.  A conservative radius
bound for the generated bubble is

\[
  R_\Sigma \le 2p+b.
\]

The production implementation should construct the exact graph closure from
the FC3 keys and requested Green-block graph; the scalar bound is only a safe
planning number.  This avoids both the current hard output pin and unnecessary
blocks absent from the real vertex graph.

For both CNT and Si in the current archives, `p=1,b=3`, hence the conservative
bound is five.  A crucial coverage correction is:

> A non-overlapping BTD reblock of `c` primitive cells reaches distance
> `2c-1`, but it does not contain every pair out to that distance.  Covering a
> complete primitive block band of radius `R` with one such BTD layout requires
> `c >= R`.

Thus a three-cell reblock is not a complete band-five reference.  The choices
are a five-cell reblock, a coverage-proven overlapping/variable-band layout, or
preferably a block-banded sparse/selected-inversion path.  Selected inversion
is established for computing requested inverse entries of structured sparse
matrices ([Lin et al.](https://doi.org/10.1137/09077432X)); here the requested
entry graph is the FC3 closure rather than a dense inverse.

The prepared `cluster/sifilm12x3` bed is consequently useful as another
reblocked production point, but it is **not** the maximum-fidelity band-five
oracle for a longer film.  Treating it as that oracle would repeat the output
coverage mistake the mixed strategy is meant to remove.

For block half-bandwidth `R`, cell DOF `d`, and length `N`, a direct block-band
factor/selected inverse has the planning scaling

\[
  \text{memory}=O(NR d^2),\qquad
  \text{factor work}=O(NR^2 d^3),
\]

up to implementation-dependent constants and q/frequency batching.  This is
linear in film length at fixed physical support and avoids inflating every
cell DOF as a five-cell reblock would.

Only after this exact pattern is generated should a tail be considered.  It
must be compressed during generation, with upper/lower generators tied by
adjoint symmetry.  Post-hoc Si HODLR is already rejected: rank 41--46 of 48 at
`1e-3`, with 0.958--1.092 times the exact-reblock storage.  Hierarchical Dyson
compression can be powerful when off-diagonal blocks are actually low rank
([Kaye and Golez](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06511)), but the measured Si
distance-2/3 blocks are high-rank near field.  Directional hierarchical
compression is relevant only if a genuine oscillatory far field remains after
lead-mode phase extraction
([Borm](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M124280X)); it cannot repair an omitted exact
near shell.

### Structure-specific spatial outcome

* **CNT:** keep the measured 8x2/band-3 state because its current spread is
  `5.04e-4`; use shell-current probes to decide whether a longer device needs a
  wider graph.  Do not return to `g_band=1`.  The conservative radius-five
  calculation remains a validation experiment, not a prerequisite for using
  the already-certified result.
* **Si L8 and longer:** L8x2 improves conservation but still has spread
  `2.83e-3`.  It covers the complete primitive band through distance two and
  only part of distance three.  Complete graph shells 3--5 with an explicit
  block-band selected solve, then determine the smallest current-certified
  subset.  At fixed certified width this remains linear in film length, making
  it substantially more attractive than wider supercells for L16 and beyond.
* **MoS2:** the stored FC3 keys are onsite (`p=0`).  With `b=1`, the generated
  support bound is already one; no spatial approximation is indicated.  Its
  immediate problem is fixed-point/frequency convergence, not long-range
  spatial compression.

## FC3 is a selected action, not one universal factorization

The finite-device archives rule out a single best vertex format:

* CNT primitive FC3 is only 2.23 % dense.  A flat rank-256 factor still has
  4.52 % error, whereas the exact atom-sparse GH200 kernel was measured at
  0.521 times the dense action time.  Use exact sparse contraction and ring
  reuse.
* Si primitive FC3 is 30.0 % dense and its tensor factors have been validated,
  including exact reblocking.  Keep the tensor-factor action.
* MoS2 is 12.55 % dense and onsite.  Benchmark the exact sparse action against
  dense before deciding; no approximation should be trained on the current
  unconverged interacting state.

All three can implement the same internal operation
`contract(vertex, G_left, G_right, output_graph)`.  This preserves the existing
production tensor speedups while letting sparse structures avoid an inaccurate
factor fit.

## Production architecture

The least disruptive implementation is four private interfaces, with no public
configuration until the gates pass:

```text
FrequencyBasis
  uniform_cells
  adaptive_cells
  passive_clusters
  reconstruct(), adjoint_project(), connection_weights()

SpatialPattern
  requested_G_graph
  exact_Sigma_graph = fc3_closure(requested_G_graph)
  selected_inverse_blocks()

FC3Action
  tensor_factor | atom_sparse | dense_oracle

MixedSCBAState
  cell_coefficients
  auxiliary_sources
  fixed_basis_epoch
  conservation_residuals
```

The main loop then becomes:

```text
certify reference / current basis
        |
freeze FrequencyBasis and SpatialPattern for one SCBA epoch
        |
selected G blocks -> one mixed bilinear bubble -> mixed Sigma state
        |
Dyson selected solve -> adjoint projection -> mix all coefficients together
        |
converged? -- no --> continue same epoch
        |
probe omitted frequency cells and spatial shells
        |
enrich only failed indicators; restart mixer history
```

MPI q distribution remains unchanged.  Uniform `C-C` terms retain the current
FFT and tensor-decomposed SSE kernels.  Adaptive connection lists and auxiliary
states should be batched over the same q ownership; only compact coefficient
metadata must be replicated.  For long Si, spatial factor data should be
streamed/cached by level or frequency batch rather than holding every expanded
factor action simultaneously.

## Required decision experiments

### Phase A: exact mixed baseline

1. Add a private block-banded output/selected-inverse path accepting the exact
   FC3 closure graph.
2. On CNT L16 and Si L8, sweep exact shell subsets and record current, lead
   balance, internal spread, memory, and iteration time.
3. Run at least Si L16 at the smallest certified graph.  Require linear memory
   growth and current error below `1e-3` relative to the widest feasible exact
   reference.

This phase answers the spatial question without relying on compression.

### Phase B: adaptive frequency cells

1. Implement P1 nested cells and sparse mixed connection weights while keeping
   the uniform FFT base.
2. Compare the complete cell-cell bubble with dense quadrature, including
   combination-frequency refinement indicators.
3. Close SCBA on CNT and Si L8; then test Si L16 and MoS2 only after its dense
   reference converges.

Require bubble error at most `2e-3`, no new symmetry/positivity defect above
`1e-10`, conservation no worse than ten times the dense reference, and end-to-
end wall time below the matched spatial baseline.  The current observable pilot
is not sufficient for this gate.

### Phase C: selective passive clusters

1. Rank candidates by FC3-weighted bubble contribution, not pole linewidth.
2. Fit a positive multi-anchor source and carry its combination outputs as
   auxiliary states.
3. Promote greedily until the next cluster fails error or break-even cost;
   refine local cells for every rejected candidate.
4. Validate first on Si L8, then transfer the same gate—not the same pole set—to
   Si L16 and other structures.

### Phase D: optional spatial tail

Only if Phase A measures a material tail outside the certified graph, build its
SSS/HSS generators directly inside the FC3 contraction.  Stop if hierarchical
rank or solve cost does not beat the block-band path.  The existing real-Si
post-hoc HODLR result already fails this gate.

## What “works across structures” should mean

It should mean the same invariants, estimators, and interfaces make a different
safe choice for each structure.  It should not mean CNT, Si, and MoS2 share one
pole count, spatial range, FC3 rank, or discretization.  A useful success
criterion is:

* one code path and error budget;
* exact Keldysh symmetry and controlled positivity by construction;
* a converged uncompressed reference before approximation training;
* automatic fallback from rational clusters to local cells and from spatial
  compression to an exact block band;
* cost governed by occupied adaptive cells, accepted auxiliary rank, and a
  current-certified spatial graph—not by inverse linewidth or total film
  length squared.

Under that definition, a mixed approach can plausibly reproduce CNT 8x2-class
conservation more cheaply and extend to long Si.  The repo evidence does not
yet establish the end-to-end speedup: the block-banded solve and mixed
cell-bubble must be implemented and closed in SCBA before making that claim.
